## 6. Discussion

**What ADCC-CP changes.** Prior conformal corrections for molecular covariate shift all estimate a covariate density ratio and plug it in as importance weights; their validity is only as good as that estimate, which is the documented failure mode in high-dimensional fingerprint space.¹⁴,²⁹ ADCC-CP reframes the problem: rather than ask *which* shift occurred and estimate it, it guarantees coverage for *every* shift in a chemically motivated family at once. The family — piecewise-linear density ratios along the applicability-domain axis — encodes the realistic deployment scenario (a library enriched for novel chemistry, by an unknown amount) and is low-dimensional enough that the finite-sample guarantee is tight. The price is a modest, bounded conservativeness ($O(d/n)$ over-coverage) and wider intervals for genuinely novel compounds — which is the correct behaviour, not a defect.

**When it suffices, and when it cannot.** Proposition 2 is the honest boundary: if the real shift has a component orthogonal to the AD axis, ADCC-CP (like any AD-based method) leaves a coverage gap bounded by $\delta_{\mathcal F}$, the AD-approximation distance. Our panel shows $\delta_{\mathcal F}\ll\delta_0$ for temporal shift — AD alignment is a good description of how ChEMBL chemistry drifts over time — so the method works; the scaffold-severe cases show the opposite, and the same diagnostic flags them. This replaces a binary "shift is too large" heuristic with a quantitative, label-free statement about *which kind* of shift a pool exhibits.

**Practical use.** For prospective screening, compute the AD calibration curve once from the labeled calibration set and the unlabeled library, read off a similarity-indexed interval for every compound, and compute $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ on the pool to check that AD-aligned correction is appropriate. Where $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ is large, the honest action is to measure rather than trust a narrow interval. The method is model-agnostic, adds seconds of CPU, and scales to libraries of any size by lookup.

**Limitations.** (i) The guarantee is group-/shift-conditional over $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$, not pointwise — unattainable distribution-free.¹⁶ (ii) Coverage degrades, with a measurable bound, under shift not aligned with the AD axis; richer classes (e.g. adding a physicochemical coordinate or a learned embedding) are a direct extension of the same framework and are future work. (iii) The AD coordinate is max-Tanimoto on ECFP4; other structural coordinates behave similarly (Supporting Information), but learned-embedding coordinates were outside the CPU-only budget. (iv) Temporal order is proxied by document year; we mitigate with single-assay targets and three windows but cannot eliminate re-reporting of older scaffolds. (v) We study regression; the framework extends to classification via the same augmented calibration.

## 7. Conclusions

Conformal prediction promises trustworthy applicability domains for molecular models but loses its guarantee under the covariate shift of prospective deployment, exactly where novel compounds are under-covered. We introduced ADCC-CP, which calibrates over a class of applicability-domain shifts and so produces intervals that are provably valid — in finite samples and without estimating any density ratio — marginally, locally at every similarity level, and under every AD-aligned deployment shift, with a bound on what non-aligned shift can cost. Across thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets under temporal shift it restores conditional coverage better than stratified and estimated-weight conformal, matching the strongest estimate-based method while carrying the guarantee that method lacks, and it yields an interpretable, library-scale applicability-domain calibration curve. The approach gives practitioners not a heuristic but a certified, inspectable reliability schedule — and a label-free diagnostic for when it applies — to support safer decisions in computational drug discovery.
