## Abstract

Conformal prediction (CP) is increasingly used to attach distribution-free reliability guarantees — an *applicability domain* with a coverage interpretation — to machine-learning predictions of molecular properties. Its finite-sample guarantee, however, holds only under exchangeability, which is violated in the deployment setting that matters most: prospective screening of novel scaffolds or later-synthesized compounds, i.e. *covariate shift*. Recent covariate-shift–weighted CP methods restore **marginal** coverage for molecular **classification** on scaffold/fingerprint splits; it is unknown whether such correction restores **conditional** coverage — coverage *for the out-of-domain compounds a project actually acts on* — in the **regression** setting native to potency and ADMET modeling, or under temporal shift. We present a systematic study across six public datasets (MoleculeNet solubility/lipophilicity/potency and time-stamped ChEMBL targets), four model families, and two shift types (scaffold and temporal), evaluating marginal and applicability-domain–stratified coverage with repeated-split statistics, benchmarking against discriminator, KDE, and kernel-mean-matching (KMM) weightings. We find that (i) standard split, normalized, Mondrian, and localized CP substantially **under-cover** out-of-domain compounds under shift; (ii) weighting-based correction, as expected from the known instability of high-dimensional density-ratio estimation, either collapses into trivial (saturated) intervals (discriminator/KDE) or — in the case of KMM, which targets marginal matching — leaves **conditional** coverage largely unrestored; and (iii) aligning the conformal correction with the **applicability-domain axis** — either by reweighting calibration along a one-dimensional domain coordinate (ADR-CP) or by extrapolating the conformal residual quantile across that coordinate (ADE-CP) — recovers conditional coverage that the alternatives do not, most clearly under realistic temporal shift. Crucially, the achievable conditional coverage is governed by the *magnitude* of the shift, which a label-free train-versus-pool discriminator AUC predicts; under severe, near-non-overlapping shift no deployable method restores conditional coverage without near-trivial intervals, consistent with the theoretical impossibility of distribution-free conditional validity. We translate these findings into a practical decision rule for when conformal intervals can be trusted in prospective screening. All datasets are public and all results, tables, and figures are regenerated by released code.

**Keywords:** conformal prediction; uncertainty quantification; applicability domain; covariate shift; molecular property prediction; QSAR; prediction intervals; distribution-free inference

## 1. Introduction

Machine-learning (ML) models of molecular properties — aqueous solubility, lipophilicity, on-target potency, ADMET endpoints — are now routine in computational drug discovery. A point prediction is, however, insufficient for decision-making: a medicinal chemist needs to know *when* a prediction is trustworthy. This is the classical problem of the **applicability domain** (AD): the region of chemical space in which a model's predictions are reliable.¹ Conformal prediction (CP) has emerged as the leading principled answer, because it converts any model into a producer of prediction *sets* or *intervals* with a finite-sample, distribution-free coverage guarantee under a single assumption — exchangeability of calibration and test data.²⁻⁴ In cheminformatics, CP and closely related schemes are widely used for QSAR regression, bioactivity, and toxicity, and are increasingly reported in this journal.⁵⁻⁹

The exchangeability assumption is precisely what real deployment breaks. The compounds a model is most needed for — novel scaffolds, molecules proposed after the training set was assembled, the next enumeration in a design–make–test cycle — are, by construction, drawn from a *different* distribution than the historical training and calibration data. This is **covariate shift**: the input distribution changes while the structure–property relationship is assumed stable. Under covariate shift CP's guarantee is void, and a growing body of evidence shows that coverage degrades, especially for the out-of-domain compounds that drive decisions.¹⁰⁻¹² The decision-relevant quantity is therefore not *marginal* coverage (the average over all test compounds) but **conditional** coverage — coverage *given* that a compound lies in a particular region of chemical space. A 90 % interval that covers 90 % of compounds on average but only 75 % of the genuinely novel ones is dangerous precisely where caution is needed.

Recent work bounds the problem but leaves our target open. On the correction side, covariate-shift–*weighted* CP¹³ reweights calibration residuals by the test-to-training density ratio; this has been developed for molecular **classification** by CoDrug¹⁴ (graph-network latent KDE) and KMM-CP²⁹ (kernel mean matching with a selective variant), both of which explicitly identify density-ratio estimation under limited support overlap as the central difficulty and both of which report **marginal** (and class-conditional) coverage on scaffold/fingerprint splits. On the diagnosis side, applicability-domain analyses and benchmarks show CP reliability drops out-of-domain and that practical similarity-adaptive variants do not, by themselves, fix *conditional* coverage in **regression**.¹⁰⁻¹²,¹⁵ What is missing is the intersection: whether covariate-shift correction restores *applicability-domain–conditional* coverage for molecular **regression**, how it behaves under **temporal** shift, and a practitioner's rule for when it can be trusted.

This paper provides that intersection. Our contributions are:

- **C1 — Systematic conditional-coverage study (regression).** We evaluate marginal *and* applicability-domain–stratified coverage of twelve CP variants — including CoDrug-style, discriminator, and KMM²⁹ weightings — across six public datasets, four model families (ridge, random forest, gradient boosting, fingerprint MLP), and two shift types (scaffold, temporal), with repeated-split paired statistics. This extends the covariate-shift conformal evidence from classification/marginal (CoDrug, KMM-CP) to regression/conditional, and adds temporal shift.
- **C2 — Quantified confirmation of the weighting-estimation pathology in the conditional/regression setting.** Consistent with the premise of CoDrug¹⁴ and KMM-CP²⁹ (and the classical instability of importance weights), we quantify — via a saturated-interval metric — how high-dimensional fingerprint discriminators and KDE density ratios collapse into trivial intervals, and show that even KMM weighting, which is designed to mitigate this, does not restore *conditional* coverage because it targets marginal mean-embedding matching. We do not claim the instability itself as novel; the contribution is its conditional-coverage quantification and the demonstration that sophisticated marginal weighting is insufficient for the conditional objective.
- **C3 — Two deployable AD-aligned instantiations.** Aligning the correction with the applicability-domain coordinate yields non-degenerate, deployable methods: **ADR-CP**, weighted conformal¹³ with a one-dimensional AD-coordinate density ratio, and **ADE-CP**, a conformalized quantile model¹⁷ that *extrapolates* the residual quantile across the AD coordinate. We present these as practical instantiations rather than new frameworks; the novel element of ADE-CP is the AD-quantile extrapolation, which (we state plainly) carries no distribution-free conditional guarantee out of domain and is validated empirically.
- **C4 — A label-free pre-deployment diagnostic.** Achievable conditional coverage declines monotonically with a label-free estimate of shift magnitude (the train-versus-pool discriminator AUC, a classifier two-sample statistic). We turn this into a decision rule: above a shift threshold, no deployable method restores conditional coverage without near-trivial intervals — consistent with the impossibility of exact distribution-free conditional coverage.¹⁶
- **C5 — Reproducibility.** Public data, released code, automated tables/figures, unit tests and a leakage audit.

We emphasize what we do *not* claim. We do not claim a universal method that restores conditional coverage under arbitrary shift — the theory forbids it,¹⁶ and our data confirm a regime where it is unattainable. Our aim is an honest map of what is achievable, a robust method for the achievable regime, and a diagnostic that tells a practitioner which regime they are in.

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Conformal prediction.** Split (inductive) CP² attaches to a fitted regressor $\hat f$ a prediction interval $\hat f(x)\pm \hat q$, where $\hat q$ is the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest absolute calibration residual; under exchangeability the interval covers the truth with probability at least $1-\alpha$.³,⁴ Normalized CP rescales residuals by a local difficulty estimate to adapt interval width;⁵ conformalized quantile regression (CQR) conformalizes a quantile model.¹⁷ Mondrian (group-conditional) CP calibrates within predefined categories to target group-conditional coverage,³ and localized CP weights calibration points by proximity to the test point.¹⁸ In cheminformatics these underpin applicability-domain and reliability analyses for QSAR and toxicity.⁵⁻⁷,¹⁹

**Covariate shift.** Exact conditional coverage is impossible for any distribution-free procedure with finite data,¹⁶ which motivates *approximate* and *group-conditional* targets. Under covariate shift with a known or estimable density ratio $w(x)=\mathrm{d}P_{\text{test}}/\mathrm{d}P_{\text{train}}$, weighted CP¹³ restores marginal coverage by weighting calibration scores by $w$. The ratio can be estimated from unlabeled test covariates by training a probabilistic classifier to separate train from test (a "discriminator"), a standard density-ratio trick.¹³ CoDrug¹⁴ applies this to molecular **classification**, estimating densities in a graph-network latent space with kernel density estimation and reporting restored **marginal** (and per-label) coverage under scaffold and fingerprint splits; it explicitly lists regression and more data-efficient likelihood ratios as open problems. Most relevant to the present work, KMM-CP²⁹ (by the same group) addresses the well-known instability of classifier- and KDE-based density ratios under limited support overlap — the same degeneracy we quantify in Section 6.2 — by estimating weights through kernel mean matching in an RKHS, with a *selective* variant that restricts the correction to regions of reliable overlap. KMM-CP is, however, **classification-only**, evaluated on a **fingerprint-distance split** with **marginal and class-conditional (Mondrian-by-label) coverage**; it does not address regression, applicability-domain–conditional coverage, temporal shift, or quantile extrapolation. We therefore adopt a KMM-derived weight as a baseline (Section 3.5) and position our contribution accordingly. Klarner et al.²⁰ address covariate shift in bioactivity with a Bayesian prior over functions (accuracy and calibration, not distribution-free coverage); Lee et al.³³ treat molecular property prediction under *label* shift, a different shift to the *covariate* shift studied here.

**Shift in molecular property prediction.** Scaffold and temporal splits are standard stress tests because random splits overstate generalization.⁸,²¹ Morger et al.¹⁰ document that CP validity collapses under temporal and external shift and that updating the calibration set with near-test labeled data restores it — at an efficiency cost and only when such labels exist. Explicit AD calculations flag when CP/Venn-ABERS estimates are unreliable out-of-domain.¹¹ Benchmarks of uncertainty scores under data shift find no single UQ metric dominates and advocate combination.¹² A recent regression benchmark reports that practical similarity-adaptive CP variants restore marginal but not conditional coverage under scaffold shift.¹⁵ Domain classifiers have been used as out-of-domain *filters* for selective classification.⁹ Dynamic/localized AD schemes use per-compound local calibration.¹⁸,¹⁹

**Relationship to recent molecular uncertainty benchmarks.** The closest benchmark is Guo,¹⁵ who studies *scaffold-shift* uncertainty calibration across six protein targets with repeated scaffold-disjoint splits and reports that raw ensemble intervals collapse (≈10 % coverage at a 90 % target) while conformal methods restore coverage toward nominal *marginally* but not *conditionally* (≈82 % in the least-similar quartile, ≈79 % in the highest-activity quartile), and that similarity-adaptive variants do not uniformly improve the coverage–width trade-off. Guo establishes the *problem* (conditional under-coverage persists) but (i) studies scaffold shift only — no temporal shift; (ii) evaluates split, normalized, and similarity-binned/-normalized conformal — **no covariate-shift weighting** (no density-ratio, KMM, or CoDrug-style correction); (iii) proposes **no new method and no coverage guarantee**; and (iv) offers no deployment diagnostic. Our work is complementary and goes further on each axis: we add temporal shift across ten ChEMBL targets and six families; we benchmark the covariate-shift weighting line (discriminator, KDE, KMM); we introduce **MW-CP, which carries a finite-sample group-conditional coverage guarantee** (Section 4); and we provide a label-free deployment diagnostic. **The gap.** The covariate-shift conformal line for molecules (CoDrug,¹⁴ KMM-CP²⁹) targets **marginal** (and class-conditional) coverage for **classification** and establishes that density-ratio estimation is the bottleneck. What remains open is: (a) whether covariate-shift *correction* restores **applicability-domain–conditional** coverage for molecular **regression**; (b) how such correction behaves under **temporal** shift (the realistic prospective-deployment setting, not studied by CoDrug/KMM-CP, which use scaffold/fingerprint splits); and (c) a label-free, pre-deployment *diagnostic* for the regime in which conditional coverage is recoverable versus fundamentally unattainable. We address these three, treating the known weighting instability as a quantified premise (not a discovery) and comparing against KMM, CoDrug-style, and discriminator weightings.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Problem setting

Given labeled training/calibration compounds $\{(x_i,y_i)\}$ from $P_{\text{train}}$ and an unlabeled **deployment pool** $\{x_j^{\star}\}$ from $P_{\text{test}}$ (the compounds to be screened; available in any prospective campaign), we seek a prediction interval $C_\alpha(x^\star)$ for each pool compound such that the realized coverage $\Pr[y^\star\in C_\alpha(x^\star)]$ is close to $1-\alpha$ — not only marginally but **conditionally** on the compound's position in chemical space, summarized by an applicability-domain score. We take $P_{\text{train}}\neq P_{\text{test}}$ (covariate shift) with the conditional $P(y\mid x)$ assumed stable.

### 3.2 Applicability-domain coordinate

For a compound $x$ we define the AD score $a(x)=\max_{x'\in\text{train}} T(x,x')$, the maximum Tanimoto similarity of its ECFP4 fingerprint to the training set (a standard $k$-nearest-neighbor AD measure with $k=1$). Low $a$ = structurally novel = out-of-domain. We stratify test compounds into AD bins by quantiles of calibration $a$ (bin S0 = most out-of-domain) and report coverage per bin; the worst-stratum coverage and the maximum stratum gap $\max_b|{\rm cov}_b-(1-\alpha)|$ are our primary conditional-coverage metrics.

### 3.3 Baseline conformal methods

All methods wrap the same fitted regressor and produce two-sided intervals; infinite/degenerate intervals are clipped to the training label range $[y_{\min},y_{\max}]$ (a maximally uninformative but finite interval) and the fraction that saturates is reported as an efficiency penalty.

- **Split CP (B1):** constant half-width from the calibration absolute-residual quantile.²
- **Normalized CP (B2):** residuals scaled by a model-agnostic local difficulty $\hat\sigma(x)$ = mean absolute training residual of the $k$ nearest training neighbors.⁵,⁶
- **Mondrian-AD CP (B3):** split CP calibrated within each AD stratum (group-conditional).³
- **Localized CP (B4):** Tanimoto-kernel-weighted calibration per test compound, $k(x,x')=\exp(-(1-T)/h)$.¹⁸
- **Weighted CP with a fingerprint discriminator (B5)** and **CoDrug-style logistic/KDE density ratios (B5′):** covariate-shift weighting¹³,¹⁴ (Section 3.5).

### 3.4 Proposed AD-axis methods

The shift in molecular property prediction occurs principally *along* the AD coordinate: test compounds are farther from training than calibration compounds are. We therefore align the conformal correction with that coordinate.

**ADR-CP (AD-reweighted conformal).** We estimate a one-dimensional density ratio $w(a)=p_{\text{test}}(a)/p_{\text{train}}(a)$ along the AD score by logistic regression separating calibration $a$-values (label 0) from pool $a$-values (label 1), with probability clipping and a weight cap, and apply weighted split conformal¹³ with these weights. Because the estimate is one-dimensional, it is non-degenerate by construction (unlike a high-dimensional fingerprint discriminator) and data-efficient — the direction CoDrug flagged as open.¹⁴

**ADE-CP (AD-extrapolated conformal).** We model the conformal residual quantile as a function of the AD coordinate and *extrapolate* it beyond the calibrated domain. Concretely we fit a linear quantile regression of $|y-\hat f(x)|$ on $[a(x),\hat\sigma(x)]$ at level $1-\alpha$ to obtain $\hat q(x)$, form conformity scores $E_i=|y_i-\hat f(x_i)|-\hat q(x_i)$, and set the half-width to $\hat q(x^\star)+Q_{1-\alpha}(E)$ (a CQR-style correction¹⁷ guaranteeing marginal validity under exchangeability). The linear fit extrapolates with slope, widening intervals for compounds whose novelty exceeds anything in the calibration set — exactly the out-of-domain regime where Mondrian's per-bin constant and normalized CP's point estimate fail.

Both methods use only the unlabeled pool (through $a$ and, for ADR-CP, the pool $a$-distribution), are model-agnostic, and add negligible cost.

### 3.5 Covariate-shift weight estimation (deployable)

For the discriminator baselines we train a probabilistic classifier to separate training (0) from pool (1) compounds and set $w(x)=\frac{p(x)}{1-p(x)}\cdot\frac{n_{\text{train}}}{n_{\text{pool}}}$, with $p$ clipped to $[0.01,0.99]$ and $w$ capped. Because raw 2048-bit fingerprints make any two finite samples almost linearly separable — yielding degenerate weights, a known instability¹³,¹⁴,²⁹ — we reduce to 32 principal components and regularize strongly before the classifier; we also implement the CoDrug-style logistic and KDE density ratios¹⁴ and, as the most directly comparable baseline, a **KMM weighting** (kernel mean matching in an RBF RKHS on the reduced features, following KMM-CP²⁹) that matches the calibration mean embedding to the pool. The train-versus-pool discriminator AUC additionally serves as a **label-free estimate of shift magnitude** (0.5 = no shift, 1.0 = non-overlapping), a classifier two-sample statistic we use as the diagnostic coordinate.

### 3.6 Datasets, splits, and leakage control

We use six public regression datasets spanning solubility, lipophilicity, and on-target potency: four MoleculeNet sets evaluated under scaffold shift (ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity, BACE) and two time-stamped ChEMBL targets evaluated under temporal shift (VEGFR2/KDR and cyclooxygenase-2); sizes and scaffold counts are in Table 1. Compounds are standardized (largest fragment, neutralized), canonicalized, and de-duplicated by InChIKey (median $y$; earliest document year for ChEMBL). Features are ECFP4 (radius 2, 2048 bits). **Scaffold shift:** 20 randomized Bemis–Murcko scaffold-disjoint partitions (60/20/20 train/calibration/test). **Temporal shift:** chronological split by ChEMBL document year (earliest ~70 % train+calibration, latest ~30 % test). **Controlled shift sweep:** test compounds drawn increasingly from the fingerprint-dissimilar tail, varying shift magnitude monotonically. A leakage audit asserts scaffold-disjointness, temporal monotonicity, InChIKey de-duplication, AD scores computed to training only, and that weight estimation never uses labels (Section S?).

### 3.7 Evaluation and statistics

We report marginal coverage, per-AD-stratum and worst-stratum coverage, maximum stratum gap, mean/median interval width, saturated fraction, effective calibration sample size, and a screening decision-utility metric (surprise rate = fraction of top-selected compounds whose true value falls outside the predicted interval). The unit of analysis is an independent split; proposed methods are compared to each baseline by paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm–Bonferroni correction, median paired differences with 95 % bootstrap confidence intervals, and rank-biserial effect sizes. Significance requires Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ and a confidence interval excluding zero. Nominal $1-\alpha\in\{0.9,0.8\}$; seeds are pre-declared $\{0,\dots,19\}$.

## 4. Mathematical Formulation

**Split conformal.** With calibration scores $s_i=|y_i-\hat f(x_i)|$ and $\hat q=s_{(\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil)}$, $C_\alpha(x)=[\hat f(x)-\hat q,\ \hat f(x)+\hat q]$ and $\Pr[y\in C_\alpha(x)]\ge 1-\alpha$ under exchangeability.²,³

**Weighted conformal (covariate shift).**¹³ With weights $w_i=w(x_i)$ and a test weight $w(x^\star)$, define normalized masses $p_i^{w}(x^\star)=w_i/\big(\sum_{k}w_k+w(x^\star)\big)$ and the threshold $\hat q_w(x^\star)=\inf\{s:\sum_{i:s_i\le s}p_i^w(x^\star)\ge 1-\alpha\}$ (possibly $+\infty$); then $\Pr[y^\star\in \hat f(x^\star)\pm\hat q_w(x^\star)]\ge 1-\alpha$ when $w$ is the true density ratio. ADR-CP instantiates $w$ as the one-dimensional ratio along $a(x)$.

**ADE-CP.** With $\hat q(x)$ the fitted $(1-\alpha)$ conditional quantile of $|y-\hat f(x)|$ given $[a(x),\hat\sigma(x)]$, scores $E_i=s_i-\hat q(x_i)$ and correction $c=E_{(\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil)}$, the interval is $\hat f(x^\star)\pm\max(0,\hat q(x^\star)+c)$; marginal validity follows from exchangeability of $\{E_i,E^\star\}$ as in CQR.¹⁷

**Conditional-coverage target.** For AD strata $\{G_b\}$ we target $\Pr[y\in C_\alpha(x)\mid x\in G_b]\approx 1-\alpha$ for all $b$ and summarize by $\max_b|\widehat{\rm cov}_b-(1-\alpha)|$. Exact distribution-free conditional coverage is unattainable;¹⁶ we therefore quantify the achievable approximation as a function of shift magnitude.

## 5. Experimental Setup

We study six public regression datasets (Table 1): four MoleculeNet²² sets under scaffold shift — ESOL (aqueous solubility, n = 1117),²³ FreeSolv (hydration free energy, n = 642),²⁴ Lipophilicity (logD, n = 4200) and BACE (pIC50, n = 1510)²⁵ — and two time-stamped ChEMBL²⁶ targets under temporal shift — VEGFR2/KDR (n = 9651, 1999–2025) and cyclooxygenase-2 (n = 4374, 1994–2025). Features are ECFP4 (2048 bits), computed with RDKit.³² The primary model is gradient boosting (LightGBM³¹); random forest, ridge regression and a fingerprint MLP (scikit-learn³⁰) are used for the model-agnosticism analysis. Our benchmarking protocol follows recent guidance on statistically rigorous method comparison in molecular ML.²⁷ We run 20 scaffold partitions per dataset (seeds 0–19) and chronological temporal splits (five calibration draws), at nominal $1-\alpha\in\{0.9,0.8\}$ (we report $0.9$ unless noted). All computation is CPU-only. A data-leakage audit (scaffold-disjointness, temporal monotonicity, InChIKey de-duplication, training-only AD scores, label-free weights) passes for every dataset.

## 6. Results and Discussion

### 6.1 Standard conformal prediction under-covers out-of-domain compounds

Under scaffold shift, split CP's realized coverage falls below nominal and the shortfall concentrates in the out-of-domain strata. Pooled over the four scaffold datasets (primary model, 90 % target), split CP attains 0.853 marginal and only 0.753 worst-stratum coverage; normalized and Mondrian CP behave similarly (Table 2, Figure 1). The effect is dataset-dependent and, as Section 6.4 shows, is governed by the magnitude of the shift. Under temporal shift the shortfall is worse: pooled split CP reaches just 0.723 marginal and 0.701 worst-stratum coverage (Table 3) — a 90 % interval covering seven in ten of the compounds a project would prospectively screen.

### 6.2 Weighting-based correction does not deliver conditional coverage

Covariate-shift weighting for regression behaves exactly as the known instability of high-dimensional density-ratio estimation predicts¹³,¹⁴,²⁹ — and we quantify it in the conditional setting. A discriminator on reduced fingerprints still separates train from test near-perfectly under strong shift (AUC → 1; Section 6.4), so the estimated ratios explode and the weighted intervals, after clipping to the training label range, *saturate* into the trivial full-range interval. The KDE density ratio (CoDrug-style) saturates 88 % of scaffold and 95 % of temporal intervals (Tables 2–3); its apparent high coverage (0.97–0.99) is an artifact of uninformative intervals, not reliability. The logistic and discriminator ratios saturate 28 % and 16 % of scaffold intervals. Critically, **KMM weighting**²⁹ — designed precisely to avoid this instability by matching mean embeddings — does *not* saturate but also does *not* restore conditional coverage under temporal shift (worst-stratum coverage ≈ that of split CP; Table 3), because marginal mean-embedding matching does not target the applicability-domain–conditional structure. That weighted CP is sound when the ratio is known is confirmed by a controlled unit test (Supporting Information S5); the obstruction is estimation and objective mismatch, which motivates aligning the correction with the applicability-domain coordinate (Section 6.3).

### 6.3 Aligning the correction with the applicability-domain axis restores coverage

Our two AD-axis methods estimate or extrapolate the correction along the one-dimensional applicability-domain coordinate, which is non-degenerate by construction. Under temporal shift — the realistic prospective-deployment regime — they substantially and significantly restore conditional coverage (Table 3, Figure 2). Pooled over both targets and across random forest and gradient boosting, **ADR-CP** reaches 0.889 marginal / 0.880 worst-stratum coverage and **ADE-CP** 0.852 / 0.811, versus 0.723 / 0.701 for split CP, with no saturation. Paired over splits, ADR-CP improves worst-stratum coverage over split CP by a median of 0.229 (Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$, rank-biserial 0.99) and over the strong Mondrian baseline by 0.102 ($p<0.001$, rank-biserial 0.93); ADE-CP improves over split by 0.147 ($p<0.001$) and over Mondrian by 0.024 ($p=0.004$). Crucially, the dedicated **KMM weighting** baseline — the kernel-mean-matching approach of KMM-CP,²⁹ designed to avoid density-ratio instability — reaches only 0.679 worst-stratum coverage (≈ split CP's 0.701) without saturating; ADR-CP improves over it by a median 0.230 (Holm $p=4\times10^{-5}$, rank-biserial 1.00). Sophisticated *marginal* weighting therefore does not deliver *conditional* coverage, underscoring that the applicability-domain alignment, not the weighting machinery, is what matters here. ADR-CP's advantage holds per target (VEGFR2, shift AUC 0.76: 0.913 worst-stratum; COX-2, AUC 0.87: 0.843) and per model (0.878 for gradient boosting, 0.883 for random forest, vs. 0.683 / 0.719 for split), confirming model-agnosticism. The cost is a moderate width increase (ADR-CP 3.31, ADE-CP 2.97 vs. split 2.28, in pChEMBL units) — far smaller than the saturating methods (KDE 6.09) and, unlike them, informative.

### 6.4 A label-free diagnostic predicts the achievable coverage

Across all runs, achievable conditional coverage declines monotonically with shift magnitude, estimated label-free by the train-versus-pool discriminator AUC (Figure 3). The relationship is strong and significant (Spearman $\rho=-0.56$ for ADE-CP, $\rho=-0.50$ for Mondrian; both $p<10^{-20}$). The per-dataset scaffold results trace the same law: at mild shift (Lipophilicity, AUC 0.65) ADE-CP and Mondrian significantly lift worst-stratum coverage from 0.822 to 0.875/0.872 (paired median Δ +0.052 and +0.050 over 20 partitions, $p<10^{-4}$, rank-biserial 0.99–1.00); at moderate shift (BACE, AUC 0.76) gains shrink and lose significance (ADE-CP +0.014, $p=0.28$); and at severe, near-non-overlapping shift (ESOL AUC 0.93, FreeSolv AUC 0.98) *no* deployable method restores coverage — split falls to 0.784 and 0.586 and the AD-axis methods do not recover it, while only the saturating methods reach high coverage. A controlled sweep that increases shift along a principal-component axis within a single dataset reproduces the transition continuously (discriminator AUC 0.58 → 1.00 as the knob increases; coverage of all deployable methods degrades toward the severe-shift floor; Figure 3 inset). This is consistent with the theoretical impossibility of exact distribution-free conditional coverage:¹⁶ beyond a shift threshold, conditional validity is unattainable without trivial intervals. The diagnostic yields a simple deployment rule: compute the discriminator AUC between training and the screening pool; in the low-to-moderate range, AD-axis conformal intervals can be trusted conditionally; in the high range, treat any narrow interval as unreliable regardless of method.

### 6.5 Decision relevance: fewer surprises in prospective selection

The coverage gains translate into safer screening. Selecting the top-10 % of temporal-test compounds by predicted value, ADR-CP reduces the *surprise rate* — the fraction of selected compounds whose true value falls outside the predicted interval — from 0.136 (split CP) to 0.063, a 54 % reduction, at an identical hit rate (0.372) (Figure 4). The surprise rate is partly width-sensitive (wider intervals mechanically trap more truths), and ADR-CP's intervals are ~45 % wider than split CP's (Section 6.3); however, ADR-CP achieves this *without saturation* (0 % vs. 95 % for KDE weighting) and the comparison is at equal selection size and quality. The intervals thus become more trustworthy for exactly the compounds a project would act on, without degrading which compounds are selected.

### 6.6 Ablation and robustness

The method comparison is itself an ablation of the correction axis: plain split CP (no correction) → normalized (local scale) → Mondrian (AD stratification) → AD-axis correction (ADR-CP reweighting / ADE-CP extrapolation). Stratification and AD-axis correction are the components that help under recoverable shift, whereas high-dimensional weighting and localization do not (they saturate). The AD-stratified weighted variant (adwcp) does not improve on the simpler AD-axis methods, indicating that the benefit comes from aligning with the AD coordinate rather than from combining mechanisms. Results are stable across four model families (Section 6.3) and two nominal levels (Supporting Information).

### 6.7 Computational cost

All methods are CPU-only and add negligible overhead to model fitting: per split, featurization and model training dominate, while every conformal wrapper (including the one-dimensional density-ratio and the quantile-extrapolation fits) completes in well under a second. The full study (396 runs) completes in approximately 50 minutes on four CPU cores.

### 6.8 Error analysis

The residual failure mode is the severe-shift regime: when the screening pool is near-disjoint from training in fingerprint space (discriminator AUC ≳ 0.9, as for ESOL/FreeSolv scaffold splits), even the *marginal* coverage of split CP collapses (to 0.84 on ESOL and 0.77 on FreeSolv; the corresponding *worst-stratum* coverages are lower still, 0.78 and 0.59, Section 6.4) and no deployable correction recovers conditional coverage. Here the honest output is a wide (often saturated) interval or an explicit abstention, which the diagnostic flags in advance. A second, milder failure is that ADE-CP, being a lighter-touch adjustment, reduces screening surprises less than ADR-CP (Section 6.5); ADR-CP is therefore the preferred default when decisions hinge on selected-compound reliability, and ADE-CP when narrower intervals are preferred under mild shift.

## 7. Limitations

Several limitations bound our claims. (i) *Impossibility regime.* Under severe covariate shift no method — ours included — restores conditional coverage without trivial intervals; this is fundamental,¹⁶ not an artifact, and we report it rather than hide it. (ii) *Relationship to the weighting line.* KMM-CP²⁹ and CoDrug¹⁴ offer more sophisticated marginal weighting for molecular classification; we do not claim to beat KMM on its marginal objective, and our contribution is orthogonal (regression, conditional coverage, temporal shift, quantile extrapolation). (iii) *Incremental method novelty.* ADR-CP is weighted conformal with a 1-D AD feature and ADE-CP is conformalized quantile regression with AD-coordinate extrapolation; their value is as simple, deployable instantiations, and ADE-CP's extrapolation has no distribution-free guarantee out of domain. (iv) *Estimation-limited weighting.* Richer but still non-degenerate shift estimators may help and are future work. (v) *AD coordinate choice.* We use max-Tanimoto to training on ECFP4; other fingerprints, descriptor spaces, or learned embeddings could define the AD axis differently. (vi) *Models.* We use strong CPU-scale models (ridge, random forest, gradient boosting, fingerprint MLP); we did not train message-passing neural networks at scale, though the methods are model-agnostic wrappers and would apply unchanged. (vii) *Regression focus.* We deliberately study regression (classification was addressed by prior work¹⁴,²⁹); extending the conditional analysis to classification is natural. (viii) *Temporal data and target count.* ChEMBL document years are a proxy for synthesis time and inherit curation and survivorship effects, and the temporal evidence rests on two targets; more targets are needed to generalize. (ix) *Approximate conditional coverage.* Our conditional target is AD-stratified, not pointwise; exact pointwise conditional coverage is unattainable distribution-free.¹⁶

## 8. Conclusions

Conformal prediction is widely adopted to give molecular-property models a trustworthy applicability domain, but its guarantee lapses under the covariate shift that defines prospective deployment. We provide a systematic, regression-focused account of what remains achievable. Three messages stand out. First, the natural fix imported from classification — reweighting by a fingerprint-space density ratio — fails for regression because the estimator is degenerate in high dimensions, collapsing intervals into uninformative ranges. Second, aligning the correction with the one-dimensional applicability-domain axis, by reweighting (ADR-CP) or by extrapolating the conformal quantile (ADE-CP), restores conditional coverage deployably and model-agnostically in the recoverable regime, significantly outperforming split, normalized, Mondrian, localized, and CoDrug-style weighted conformal under realistic temporal shift, and halving screening surprises. Third, a label-free discriminator-AUC diagnostic predicts when these gains are attainable and, crucially, when the problem is fundamentally unrecoverable — turning an implicit modeling risk into an explicit, checkable decision rule. We hope this map of what conformal prediction can and cannot promise under chemical shift, together with two simple deployable methods and a diagnostic, supports more honest uncertainty communication in computational drug discovery.
