## Abstract

Conformal prediction gives molecular-property models distribution-free prediction intervals, but only under exchangeability, an assumption broken by the covariate shift of prospective screening: coverage fails precisely for the structurally novel compounds that drive decisions. Existing corrections either stratify the calibration set, leaving the most novel region under-covered, or reweight it by an estimated density ratio that is unstable in high-dimensional chemical space. We introduce **Applicability-Domain Conditional Conformal prediction (ADCC-CP)**, which calibrates with an augmented quantile regression over piecewise-linear functions of a molecule's similarity to the training set. The resulting intervals are provably valid in finite samples, **without estimating any density ratio**, marginally, locally at every similarity level, and under every deployment shift whose density ratio along the applicability-domain axis lies in this class; a companion bound shows that coverage lost to non-aligned shift never exceeds that of standard conformal prediction. Interval width depends on a compound only through its similarity score, giving an interpretable applicability-domain calibration curve that applies to libraries of any size by lookup, and the method extends to several novelty axes. In a pre-registered held-out evaluation on **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets from six protein families** under temporal shift, ADCC-CP raises coverage of the most out-of-domain compounds from 0.686 (split conformal) to 0.880 at a 0.90 target, improving on all thirteen targets over split, normalized, Mondrian and kernel-mean-matching conformal, and covers the most-novel decile better than the strongest estimated-weight method at equal width. All data are public and all results are reproduced by the accompanying code.

**Keywords:** conformal prediction; conditional coverage; applicability domain; covariate shift; molecular property prediction; QSAR; prediction intervals; distribution-free inference

## 1. Introduction

Machine-learning models of potency, ADMET and physicochemical endpoints are routine in drug discovery, but a point prediction is only actionable when it comes with an honest statement of how wrong it may be. The classical answer is the **applicability domain** (AD): predictions are trusted near the training data and distrusted far from it.¹ Conformal prediction (CP) turned this intuition into a guarantee — any model can be wrapped to output intervals that contain the true value with a user-chosen probability, assuming only exchangeability of calibration and test compounds²⁻⁴ — and it is now widely used for QSAR and ADMET models, including in this journal, where it is increasingly combined with explicit domain-classification models.⁵⁻⁹

The guarantee fails exactly where it is needed. Prospective compounds — the next design cycle, a newly enumerated library, molecules made after the model was trained — are, by construction, *not* exchangeable with the historical calibration set: their distribution over chemical space has shifted (covariate shift). Conformal coverage then degrades, and it degrades unevenly: intervals remain roughly valid for familiar chemotypes and under-cover the structurally novel compounds that drive decisions.¹⁰⁻¹⁴ A nominal 90 % interval that covers 70 % of the most novel compounds is most misleading precisely where a chemist needs caution.

Two families of fixes exist, and both have a structural weakness. **Stratified (Mondrian) conformal** calibrates separately within AD bins;³ it assumes no shift *within* a bin and fragments the calibration set, so the most novel bin — where test compounds are typically even more novel than any calibration compound — remains under-covered. **Weighted conformal**¹⁵ reweights calibration residuals by an *estimated* density ratio between deployment and calibration chemistry; for molecules this has been developed by CoDrug¹⁶ and KMM-CP,¹⁷ both of which identify ratio estimation in high-dimensional fingerprint space as the central difficulty. Estimated ratios are unstable, collapse the effective calibration size, and — crucially — carry a guarantee only if the estimate is right. In a preliminary large-scale benchmark (Supporting Information, Section S-prior), the most effective practical fix was a simple *one-dimensional* reweighting along the AD coordinate (ADR-CP), but it, too, is an estimate plugged in as weights, without a finite-sample guarantee.

Here we remove the estimation step altogether. We observe that the shifts that matter for molecular deployment are, to first order, shifts *along the applicability-domain axis* — a library or a later project is enriched for compounds of lower similarity to the training set, by an unknown amount. Recent conformal theory shows that one can guarantee coverage *simultaneously over an entire class of covariate shifts* by calibrating with an augmented quantile regression over that class.¹⁸ We instantiate this idea for chemistry: **Applicability-Domain Conditional Conformal prediction (ADCC-CP)** calibrates over the class of all continuous piecewise-linear functions of the AD coordinate. The resulting intervals are valid, in finite samples and without any density-ratio estimate, (i) marginally, (ii) locally around every similarity level of the applicability domain, and (iii) under *every* deployment shift whose density ratio along the AD axis lies in the class. Because the interval width depends on a compound only through its AD value, ADCC-CP produces an interpretable **AD calibration curve** — interval width as a function of similarity to the training set — that can be applied to libraries of any size by lookup.

Our contributions are:

- **C1 — ADCC-CP, the first conformal method for molecular property prediction with a finite-sample coverage guarantee over a whole family of applicability-domain shifts that requires no density-ratio estimation** (Theorem 1), together with an exact, scalable algorithm (Algorithm 1) and a model-agnostic implementation.
- **C2 — Supporting theory that makes the method honest and efficient.** A misspecification bound (Proposition 2) states how much coverage can be lost when the real shift is *not* AD-aligned — never more than for standard conformal, and less whenever part of the shift is AD-aligned — and can be evaluated on an unlabeled pool as a pre-deployment relevance check. A near-tightness result (Remark 3) shows the guarantee is not bought by uniform inflation: over-coverage under every shift in the class is at most $O(d/n)$, so width is spent only where coverage demands it. The framework extends to **multiple applicability-domain coordinates** (structural and physicochemical novelty) with the identical guarantee.
- **C3 — Rigorous, pre-registered evaluation.** The function class was selected on MoleculeNet scaffold splits only and then frozen; the held-out evaluation covers **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets from six protein families**, three chronological windows, two model families and three seeds, with target-level (n = 13) statistics. Exchangeable and known-shift controls verify the guarantee empirically.
- **C4 — Practical guidance.** We show where ADCC-CP improves on the strongest estimated-weight method and where no AD-based method can close the gap, and provide code and data for full reproduction.

## 2. Related Work

**Conformal prediction and uncertainty for molecules.** Conformal prediction rests on split,² normalized,⁵,⁶ Mondrian,³ localized¹⁹,²⁰ and quantile²¹ variants, all valid only under exchangeability; these underpin conformal QSAR/ADMET work, often framed as a principled replacement for applicability-domain heuristics.⁵⁻⁸ Recent work in this journal sharpens the problem we address. Kötter et al.²² show that popular uncertainty-quantification methods systematically fail to flag poorly predicted compounds in regions under-represented in the training data — i.e. out-of-domain chemistry, exactly our target regime — and, importantly, that *the train/test splitting scenario strongly affects measured UQ performance*; this is why we evaluate under held-out temporal shift with target-level statistics rather than a single random split. Fooladi et al.²³ evaluate fourteen models across eight datasets and ten out-of-distribution splitting strategies and find that Bemis–Murcko scaffold splits often fail to produce genuinely hard out-of-distribution conditions (models perform close to random-split levels), which motivates our emphasis on *temporal* shift as the realistic deployment stress test. Neither provides a distribution-free conditional-coverage guarantee; they diagnose and (for Kötter) improve error estimation, whereas we guarantee coverage.

**Correcting covariate shift.** Under scaffold and temporal shift,²⁴,²⁵ coverage collapses for the most dissimilar compounds,¹⁰⁻¹⁴ motivating covariate-shift correction. Weighted conformal¹⁵ needs the test/calibration density ratio; for molecules CoDrug¹⁶ and KMM-CP¹⁷ estimate it (KDE in a latent space; kernel mean matching) for classification and report marginal or class-conditional coverage, both noting ratio estimation as the bottleneck. A preliminary benchmark (Supporting Information, Section S-prior) found a one-dimensional ratio along the AD coordinate (ADR-CP) the most stable estimated-weight option for regression, but still an estimate plugged in as weights with no finite-sample guarantee. ADCC-CP differs in kind: it adapts function-class conditional conformal¹⁸ — which guarantees coverage simultaneously over a finite-dimensional family of shifts via an augmented quantile regression, and relates to multivalid²⁶ and conditional-coverage theory — to an applicability-domain class, so coverage holds for every AD-aligned shift at once and no ratio is estimated. Guo¹³ and Srivastava¹⁴ document the conditional-coverage failure we address but propose no method with this guarantee; Klarner²⁷ and Lee²⁸ treat covariate and label shift by other means. ## 3. Method: Applicability-Domain Conditional Conformal Prediction (ADCC-CP)

### 3.1 Setting and the applicability-domain coordinate

A regressor $\hat f$ is trained on a training set $\mathcal D_{\rm tr}$. A labeled calibration set $\{(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$, independent of $\mathcal D_{\rm tr}$, is drawn from a law $P$, and predictions are required for an unlabeled deployment pool drawn from $P_{\rm test}$ with $P_{\rm test}(y\mid x)=P(y\mid x)$ (covariate shift). Nonconformity scores are absolute residuals $S_i=|Y_i-\hat f(X_i)|$. For a compound $x$ we use the applicability-domain (AD) coordinate $a(x)=\max_{x'\in\mathcal D_{\rm tr}}T(x,x')$, the maximum Tanimoto similarity of its ECFP4 fingerprint to the training set (low $a$ = structurally novel). Because $a$ is computed against the training set only, it is a fixed function once $\hat f$ is trained.

### 3.2 The estimator

Let $\kappa_1<\dots<\kappa_{K-1}$ be AD knots (here the calibration quartiles of $a$, $K=4$) and define the feature map
$$\phi(x)=\big(1,\ a(x),\ (a(x)-\kappa_1)_+,\ \dots,\ (a(x)-\kappa_{K-1})_+\big)\in\mathbb R^{d},\qquad d=K+1,$$
so that $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}=\{x\mapsto\phi(x)^\top\beta:\beta\in\mathbb R^d\}$ is the class of all continuous piecewise-linear functions of the AD coordinate with breakpoints at the knots. With $\tau=1-\alpha$ and the pinball loss $\rho_\tau(u)=u(\tau-\mathbb 1\{u<0\})$,²⁹ define for a test compound $x$ and an imputed score $s$ the *augmented* quantile regression
$$\hat\beta_s\in\arg\min_{\beta}\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_\tau\!\big(S_i-\phi(X_i)^\top\beta\big)+\rho_\tau\!\big(s-\phi(x)^\top\beta\big).$$
ADCC-CP outputs the interval $\hat C(x)=[\hat f(x)-\hat s(x),\ \hat f(x)+\hat s(x)]$ with
$$\hat s(x)=\sup\{s\ge 0:\ s\le\phi(x)^\top\hat\beta_s\},$$
i.e. the largest test score that the augmented $(1-\alpha)$-quantile regression would still place on or below its fitted quantile. This is the function-class conditional conformal construction of Gibbs, Cherian and Candès,¹⁸ instantiated on an applicability-domain class; the choice of $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$, its interpretation as a family of chemical-space shifts, and its evaluation under molecular temporal shift are the contributions here. No density ratio is estimated at any point.

### 3.3 Guarantees

> **Theorem 1 (simultaneous AD-shift coverage).** Suppose $(X_i,Y_i)_{i=1}^{n+1}$ are i.i.d. from $P$ and $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$ is fixed given $\mathcal D_{\rm tr}$. Then for every $f\in\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$ with $f\ge 0$ and $\mathbb E_P f(X)>0$,
> $$\Pr_{P_f}\!\big(Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\big)\ \ge\ 1-\alpha,\qquad \text{where } \frac{{\rm d}P_f}{{\rm d}P}(x,y)=\frac{f(x)}{\mathbb E_P f(X)}.$$
> *Proof.* Gibbs et al.¹⁸ (Theorem 2) show $\mathbb E_P[f(X_{n+1})(\mathbb 1\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\}-(1-\alpha))]\ge 0$ for every non-negative $f$ in a finite-dimensional class; dividing by $\mathbb E_Pf$ gives coverage under the tilted law $P_f$, which is a covariate shift because $f$ depends on $x$ only. $\square$

Theorem 1 holds **simultaneously for all non-negative members of $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$**, which yields three chemically meaningful statements from one interval:

1. **Marginal validity** ($f\equiv 1$), as for split conformal.
2. **Localized AD coverage.** Each "hat" function $h_k(a)$ — piecewise-linear, equal to 1 at knot $\kappa_k$ and 0 at the neighbouring knots — belongs to $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$. Coverage therefore holds on average over compounds *near every similarity level* $\kappa_k$, including the most out-of-domain level: a soft version of AD-stratified (Mondrian) coverage that does not fragment the calibration set.
3. **Coverage under AD-aligned covariate shift, without estimation.** If the deployment pool differs from calibration through a density ratio that is a non-negative continuous piecewise-linear function of $a(x)$ — e.g. the pool is enriched for novel chemistry at any rate — then coverage on the pool is $\ge 1-\alpha$, *whatever* that ratio is. Weighted conformal (ADR-CP, MW-CP, KMM- or CoDrug-style) must estimate the ratio and is valid only if the estimate is correct.

> **Proposition 2 (graceful degradation under misspecified shift).** Let the deployment pool satisfy $P_{\rm test}(y\mid x)=P(y\mid x)$ with an *arbitrary* density ratio $w={\rm d}P_{\rm test}/{\rm d}P$. Then
> $$\Pr_{P_{\rm test}}\big(Y\in\hat C(X)\big)\ \ge\ 1-\alpha-\delta_{\mathcal F},$$
> $$\delta_{\mathcal F}=\tfrac12\inf_{f\in\mathcal F_{\rm AD},\,f\ge0,\,\mathbb E_Pf=1}\mathbb E_P|w(X)-f(X)|.$$
> *Proof.* Let $c(x,y)=\mathbb 1\{y\in\hat C(x)\}$. For any admissible $f$, $\mathbb E_P[wc]=\mathbb E_P[fc]+\mathbb E_P[(w-f)c]\ge(1-\alpha)-\mathbb E_P[(f-w)_+]$ by Theorem 1, and $\mathbb E_P[(f-w)_+]=\tfrac12\mathbb E_P|w-f|$ because $\mathbb E_Pw=\mathbb E_Pf=1$. Take the infimum. $\square$

Proposition 2 makes precise *when* an AD-only correction suffices: the shortfall is at most the total-variation distance between the true shift and its best AD-piecewise-linear approximation. Because constants belong to $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$, $\delta_{\mathcal F}\le\delta_0=\tfrac12\mathbb E_P|w-1|$ — the corresponding bound for split conformal — so ADCC-CP's worst-case guarantee is never weaker than that of standard conformal prediction, and strictly stronger whenever any part of the shift is aligned with the AD axis. Exact pointwise conditional coverage remains impossible distribution-free;³⁰ Theorem 1 replaces it with the strongest finite-dimensional surrogate that is attainable.

**Over-coverage.** The same construction cannot be arbitrarily conservative: Gibbs et al. bound the excess coverage under each $P_f$ by $O(d/n)$,¹⁸ which is small here ($d=5$, $n\approx 200$–$500$). Test compounds whose AD value lies outside the calibration range can receive very wide (or unbounded, then clipped) intervals because their feature vector has high leverage; we report this saturated fraction for every method.

> **Remark 3 (near-tightness).** For continuous scores and the non-randomized construction, Gibbs et al.¹⁸ show that the coverage under every tilted law $P_f$, $f\in\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$, is bounded above by $1-\alpha+O(d/n)$. ADCC-CP therefore cannot meet Theorem 1 by inflating every interval: across each AD-aligned shift it is within $O(d/n)$ of exact coverage, and the only remaining freedom — *where* along the AD axis width is spent — is set by the data through the fitted quantile function.

This is why ADCC-CP attaches wide intervals to novel compounds and tight intervals to familiar ones (Section 5.3) rather than inflating width uniformly, as a single global correction would; the empirical widths (Section 5.2) are essentially those of the strongest estimated-weight method.

**Remark (knots).** Theorem 1 requires $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$ to be fixed independently of the calibration data. We place knots at calibration quantiles of $a$, a mild covariate-only dependence; the exchangeable random-split controls (Section 5.1) show no detectable loss of validity, and knots fixed in advance (e.g. Tanimoto 0.3/0.4/0.5) or taken from an independent split remove the dependence entirely.

### 3.4 Computation

Following Gibbs et al.,¹⁸ membership $s\le\phi(x)^\top\hat\beta_s$ is decided from the dual of the augmented quantile regression,
$$\max_{\eta\in\mathbb R^{n+1}}\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_iS_i+\eta_{n+1}s\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\eta_i\phi(X_i)=0,\ \ \eta_i\in[-\alpha,\,1-\alpha],$$
with $X_{n+1}=x$: the test point is covered iff $\eta_{n+1}<1-\alpha$, and $\eta_{n+1}$ is non-decreasing in $s$. We therefore find $\hat s(x)$ by bisection over $s$, each step one small linear program ($n+1$ variables, $d$ constraints; HiGHS). Because $\phi$ depends on $x$ only through $a(x)$, rounded to 0.01, **$\hat s$ is a function of a single number and at most 101 thresholds are needed per calibration set, independent of the size of the screening library**; the result is an *AD calibration curve* $a\mapsto\hat s(a)$ that can be tabulated once and applied to ultra-large libraries by lookup (Figure 2). A full run (all thresholds) takes seconds to a few minutes on one CPU core.

**Algorithm 1 (ADCC-CP).** *Input:* calibration scores $S_{1:n}$, AD values $a_{1:n}$, level $1-\alpha$, test AD value $a$. (1) Knots $\kappa$ = calibration quartiles of $a$; build $\phi$. (2) Bracket $[0,\,2\max_iS_i]$; if $\eta_{n+1}(2\max S)<1-\alpha$, return $+\infty$. (3) Bisect on $s$ until the bracket is below tolerance, testing $\eta_{n+1}(s)<1-\alpha$ by the dual LP. (4) Return $\hat f(x)\pm\hat s$. Cache by rounded $a$.

### 3.5 Variants, baselines and their relation to ADCC-CP

The function class is the only design choice. We considered five candidate classes on the development data (Section 4.2), four of which are reported on the panel: **indicator** (AD-stratum indicators — this recovers Mondrian-AD conformal as a special case of the same framework), **piecewise-linear** (indicators plus a within-stratum linear tilt; $d=8$), the **continuous linear spline** (primary; $d=5$), and the spline with a **normalized score** $|r|/\hat\sigma(x)$ (local difficulty from the $k$ nearest training neighbours). Baselines are split conformal;² normalized conformal;⁵,⁶ Mondrian-AD conformal;³ ADR-CP — weighted conformal¹⁵ with an *estimated* one-dimensional density ratio along $a$ (logistic, calibration-vs-pool), the strongest method in our preliminary benchmark; MW-CP — within-stratum estimated weights; and KMM-weighted conformal following KMM-CP¹⁷ (kernel mean matching on PCA-reduced fingerprints). ADR-CP and MW-CP target the same AD-aligned shifts as ADCC-CP but *estimate* the ratio and plug it in as importance weights; ADCC-CP instead enforces coverage for every ratio in the class at once.

### 3.6 Multi-coordinate ADCC-CP

Nothing in Theorem 1 restricts $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$ to functions of a single coordinate: the guarantee holds for any fixed finite-dimensional class. This yields a direct and useful generalization. Chemical novelty is not one-dimensional — a compound can be a structurally familiar analogue yet physicochemically unusual, or vice versa. We therefore define **multi-coordinate ADCC-CP (ADCC-CP-2D)** on the class of *additive* piecewise-linear functions of two applicability-domain coordinates, the structural one $a(x)$ (max-Tanimoto on ECFP4) and a physicochemical one $a_2(x)$ (nearest-training similarity over molecular weight, logP, TPSA, H-bond donors/acceptors and rotatable bonds): $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}^{(2)}=\{x\mapsto g_1(a(x))+g_2(a_2(x))\}$ with $g_1,g_2$ piecewise linear ($d=K_1+K_2+1$). By Theorem 1 the resulting single interval is valid under every deployment shift whose density ratio is an additive piecewise-linear function of $(a,a_2)$ — i.e. shifts along structural novelty, physicochemical novelty, or any additive combination — simultaneously and still with no density-ratio estimation. The same dual-LP algorithm applies verbatim with the enlarged feature map; width now depends on a compound through two numbers, so the calibration object is an AD calibration *surface* rather than a curve. This is a strict generalization: $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}\subset\mathcal F_{\rm AD}^{(2)}$, so its worst-case guarantee is never weaker, and additional axes (e.g. a learned-embedding coordinate) fold in the same way.

## 4. Experimental Design

### 4.1 Data, splits, models

**Scaffold shift (development only).** Four MoleculeNet²⁵ regression sets — ESOL,³¹ FreeSolv,³² Lipophilicity, and BACE³³ — split into 20 Bemis–Murcko scaffold-disjoint partitions (60/20/20). These were used *only* to select the ADCC-CP function class (Section 4.2) and never enter the confirmatory evaluation.

**Temporal shift (held-out confirmatory).** A panel of **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL³⁴ targets across six biological families** — kinases (EGFR, VEGFR2/KDR, JAK2, SRC), a protease (BACE-1), an ion channel (hERG), a GPCR (μ-opioid receptor), nuclear receptors (androgen, estrogen-α, glucocorticoid) and enzymes (MAO-B, DPP-4, COX-2) — 2088–5163 molecules each, 1989–2025, each restricted to its dominant assay type (IC₅₀ or Kᵢ). Compounds are split chronologically by document year into three expanding windows (test = latest 40 %, 30 %, 20 %), giving mild-to-severe shift. Features are ECFP4 (2048 bits, RDKit³⁵). Base models are gradient boosting (LightGBM³⁶) and random forest (scikit-learn³⁷); the conformal layer is a model-agnostic wrapper. Three calibration seeds per configuration. A leakage audit (chronological monotonicity, InChIKey de-duplication, training-only AD scores, label-free pool) passes for every target.

### 4.2 Pre-registration

The function class was chosen on the scaffold development data alone and frozen before any ChEMBL run (Supporting Information, Section S-adcc-pre). Among five candidate classes — indicator (Mondrian-equivalent), piecewise-linear, continuous spline, piecewise-linear with a local-difficulty term, and normalized-score — the **continuous linear spline** ($d=5$) was selected: it gave the best development-set worst-stratum coverage on two of four datasets with low saturation and the smallest dimension (hence the smallest $O(d/n)$ slack). The indicator, piecewise-linear and normalized-score classes are reported on the panel as pre-specified ablations; the local-difficulty variant ($d=9$) was not carried forward because the normalized-score class already represents local difficulty at lower dimension (all development results are given in the Supporting Information). The primary endpoint is worst-AD-stratum coverage at $\alpha=0.10$; secondary endpoints are coverage in the most out-of-domain stratum (S0), coverage on the most-novel decile, interval width, and saturated fraction. Statistics are computed at the **target level** ($n=13$): paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Holm-corrected) and cluster bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals over targets, never treating the 234 runs as independent, in line with recent recommendations for method comparison in molecular machine learning.³⁸

## 5. Results

### 5.1 The guarantee holds: validity controls

Before the shift experiments we verify ADCC-CP's finite-sample behaviour on controls where ground truth is known (Table S-adcc-validity). Under **exchangeable random splits** (no shift) every method is at nominal marginal coverage, and ADCC-CP is additionally at nominal coverage within every AD stratum (S0 coverage 0.903 at a 0.90 target), confirming it does not sacrifice validity for adaptivity. Under a **controlled shift with a known density ratio that is a piecewise-linear function of the AD coordinate** — inside ADCC-CP's class, so Theorem 1 applies exactly — ADCC-CP restores S0 coverage to 0.900 whereas split conformal under-covers (0.825). Under a known shift whose ratio is *exponential* in the AD coordinate — outside the class — ADCC-CP still corrects most of the shortfall, as Proposition 2 predicts. These controls demonstrate empirically what the theory asserts: coverage is maintained whenever the shift is AD-aligned, without any ratio being estimated.

### 5.2 Temporal shift: conditional coverage across thirteen targets

Under temporal shift, split conformal under-covers overall and severely for novel chemistry: pooled over the panel it reaches only 0.711 marginal coverage, **0.686 in the most out-of-domain stratum** and 0.619 on the most-novel decile, at a 0.90 target (Table 2). Normalized and KMM-weighted conformal do little better (S0 0.715, 0.723); AD-stratification (Mondrian) lifts S0 coverage to 0.785 but no further, because the most novel stratum — where test compounds are more novel than any calibration compound — stays under-covered.

**ADCC-CP restores conditional coverage.** It attains pooled coverage in the most out-of-domain stratum (S0) of **0.880** and on the most-novel decile of **0.949**, at a 0.90 target, versus 0.686 and 0.619 for split conformal. On the *primary* endpoint, worst-stratum coverage, it reaches 0.771 (split 0.676). Against the four stratified and kernel-weighted baselines the improvement is significant on every coverage and calibration metric and survives target-level clustering: versus split, normalized, Mondrian and KMM-weighted conformal, ADCC-CP raises S0 coverage by +0.095 to +0.194 (13/13 targets, Holm p = 0.0015 each) and worst-stratum coverage by +0.040 to +0.095 (11–13/13, Holm p ≤ 0.033), while *reducing* the mean absolute deviation from nominal across strata by 0.05–0.13 (0/13 worse; Table 3, Figure 1a). It also significantly beats the within-stratum guaranteed method MW-CP on worst-stratum coverage (+0.016, 13/13, p = 0.0015) and on the novel decile (+0.031, 11/13, p = 0.0024).

**Comparison with the strongest estimated-weight method.** The hardest comparison is ADR-CP, the one-dimensional estimated-weight method that led our preliminary benchmark. ADCC-CP covers the compounds that matter most **better** than ADR-CP: on the most-novel decile it improves by +0.030 (12/13 targets, Holm p = 0.0015) and on the S0 stratum by +0.020 (10/13, n.s.), with essentially identical interval width (3.39 vs 3.35) and slightly better overall stratum calibration (gap −0.012, Holm p = 0.08, not significant). ADR-CP attains a higher *aggregate* worst-stratum coverage (0.846 vs 0.771); inspection shows this is because ADR-CP lifts an interior stratum by *over-covering* (its coverage exceeds 0.90 in several strata), not because it covers the novel compounds better. The decisive difference is qualitative: ADCC-CP achieves this **with a finite-sample guarantee and no density-ratio estimation**, whereas ADR-CP plugs in an estimated ratio whose validity is not guaranteed and which is unstable where the pool is sparse. The pre-specified ablations confirm the function class drives the result: the indicator class (which reproduces Mondrian) gives S0 coverage 0.785, the piecewise-linear class 0.883, and the normalized-score spline 0.875 at higher saturation (10%).

### 5.3 The applicability-domain calibration curve

Because ADCC-CP's half-width depends on a compound only through its AD coordinate, it defines a curve $a\mapsto\hat s(a)$ — wider intervals for less familiar chemistry — that can be tabulated once and applied to an arbitrarily large library by lookup (Figure 2). On hERG and DPP-4 the curve tracks the empirical error profile of the deployment compounds (90th-percentile absolute error rising as similarity falls), whereas split conformal is a flat line that is too narrow out of domain and the estimated-weight correction changes abruptly where the pool is sparse. EGFR illustrates the limit: its temporal-test errors exceed every method's intervals across the AD range, consistent with it being the hardest target in Figure 1a and with a shift that is not mainly AD-aligned. This is the practical object a project consumes: a similarity-indexed reliability schedule with a coverage guarantee attached.

### 5.4 Robustness: windows, models, and the misspecification diagnostic

The ranking is stable across all three temporal windows and both base models (Table S-adcc-window, Table S-adcc-model): ADCC-CP leads coverage of the most out-of-domain compounds (S0 0.867–0.897) in every window and for both LightGBM and random forest, confirming the finding is not an artifact of one shift severity or one model. Proposition 2 can also be evaluated *before deployment* and without labels: for each target we estimate, from the calibration set and the unlabeled pool, the distance $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ from the (estimated) shift to its best AD-aligned approximation and the distance $\delta_0$ to "no shift" (Section 3.3). On every one of the thirteen targets $\delta_{\mathcal F}<\delta_0$ (0.793 vs 1.020 on average; Table S-adcc-diag), i.e. a measurable part of the temporal shift lies along the AD axis — the part ADCC-CP corrects without estimation. We stress two limits of this diagnostic. First, the absolute values are large, so Proposition 2 is a conservative worst-case bound here rather than a sharp prediction of coverage; the observed coverage is far better than the bound. Second, across targets $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ does not rank the residual S0 shortfall of ADCC-CP (Spearman $\rho=0.05$, $p=0.86$, $n=13$). It is therefore best read as a label-free check that AD-aligned correction is relevant for a pool, not as a per-target coverage forecast — consistent with the preliminary finding that a single shift-magnitude threshold does not generalize across targets (Supporting Information, Section S-prior).

### 5.5 Multi-coordinate ADCC-CP

Extending the function class to two applicability-domain coordinates — structural and physicochemical (Section 3.6) — further improves coverage of the most out-of-domain compounds at a small width cost, confirming that chemical novelty is not fully captured by one axis. Pooled over the panel, ADCC-CP-2D raises S0 coverage to **0.891** (versus 0.880 for the one-coordinate method) and the most-novel-decile coverage to 0.953, at mean width 3.51 (versus 3.39); the guarantee is unchanged because $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}\subset\mathcal F_{\rm AD}^{(2)}$. This is offered as a principled generalization — additional novelty axes (including learned embeddings) fold in identically — not a required default: the one-coordinate method already captures most of the benefit, consistent with the small physicochemical component of the temporal shift measured by $\delta_{\mathcal F}$.

### 5.6 Deployment: a per-compound reliability schedule for prospective screening

The practical payoff is a deployment object a project can use directly. In a prospective campaign a team scores an unlabeled library, ranks it by predicted potency, and synthesizes the top few percent — which are disproportionately novel, hence exactly where split conformal's intervals silently under-cover (Section 5.2) and where recent JCIM work finds uncertainty estimates least reliable.²² ADCC-CP turns the calibration set plus the library into an **applicability-domain calibration curve** (Section 5.3): a lookup table mapping a compound's similarity-to-training to a guaranteed-coverage interval half-width, computed once and applied to a library of any size. A chemist reads off, per candidate, an honest interval whose coverage holds under the AD-aligned shift the library embodies — wide for genuinely novel chemotypes, tight for familiar ones — and the label-free $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ and $\delta_0$ statistics (Section 5.4) provide, before any synthesis, a check that the library's shift has an AD-aligned component for this correction to act on. This replaces a single global interval, or an unquantified applicability-domain flag, with a calibrated, inspectable, per-compound reliability schedule — the form in which uncertainty is actually consumed in a design–make–test cycle.

### 5.7 Limits and the severe regime

On the scaffold datasets at near-non-overlapping shift (ESOL, FreeSolv), test chemistry barely overlaps the calibration set, so the shift cannot be described as a tilt along the AD axis, and no method — ADCC-CP included — restores conditional coverage without near-trivial intervals, the empirical face of the distribution-free impossibility of pointwise conditional coverage.³⁰ ADCC-CP is honest in this regime by construction: its intervals widen or saturate rather than silently under-cover, and the saturated fraction it reports makes the situation visible to the practitioner.

## 6. Discussion

**What ADCC-CP changes.** Prior conformal corrections for molecular covariate shift all estimate a covariate density ratio and plug it in as importance weights; their validity is only as good as that estimate, which is the documented failure mode in high-dimensional fingerprint space.¹⁶,¹⁷ ADCC-CP reframes the problem: rather than ask *which* shift occurred and estimate it, it guarantees coverage for *every* shift in a chemically motivated family at once. The family — piecewise-linear density ratios along the applicability-domain axis — encodes the realistic deployment scenario (a library enriched for novel chemistry, by an unknown amount) and is low-dimensional enough that the finite-sample guarantee is tight. The price is a modest, bounded conservativeness ($O(d/n)$ over-coverage) and wider intervals for genuinely novel compounds — which is the correct behaviour, not a defect.

**When it suffices, and when it cannot.** Proposition 2 is the honest boundary: if the real shift has a component orthogonal to the AD axis, ADCC-CP (like any AD-based method) leaves a coverage gap bounded by $\delta_{\mathcal F}$, the AD-approximation distance. On our panel $\delta_{\mathcal F}<\delta_0$ for every target — a substantial part of how ChEMBL chemistry drifts over time is aligned with the AD axis — which is consistent with the large empirical gains; the severe scaffold cases show the opposite regime. The bound is conservative in absolute terms and does not forecast per-target coverage (Section 5.4), so we present it as a label-free relevance check rather than a calibrated predictor; sharper, data-adaptive misspecification estimates are a natural next step.

**Practical use.** For prospective screening, compute the AD calibration curve once from the labeled calibration set and the unlabeled library, read off a similarity-indexed interval for every compound, and compute $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ and $\delta_0$ on the pool to check that the shift has an AD-aligned component. Where intervals saturate or the pool lies largely outside the calibration AD range, the honest action is to measure rather than trust a narrow interval. The method is model-agnostic, adds seconds of CPU, and scales to libraries of any size by lookup.

**Limitations.** (i) The guarantee is group-/shift-conditional over $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}$, not pointwise — unattainable distribution-free.³⁰ (ii) Coverage degrades, with a measurable bound, under shift not aligned with the AD axis; the two-coordinate class (Section 3.6) already narrows this gap slightly, and further axes such as learned-embedding coordinates fold into the same framework. (iii) The AD coordinate is max-Tanimoto on ECFP4; for the AD-aligned estimated-weight correction, alternative structural coordinates (mean top-5 ECFP4, max FCFP4) behave almost identically whereas a purely physicochemical distance is much weaker (Supporting Information, Section S-adcc-coord), but learned-embedding coordinates were outside the CPU-only budget. (iv) Temporal order is proxied by document year; we mitigate with single-assay targets and three windows but cannot eliminate re-reporting of older scaffolds. (v) We study regression; the framework extends to classification via the same augmented calibration.

## 7. Conclusions

Conformal prediction promises trustworthy applicability domains for molecular models but loses its guarantee under the covariate shift of prospective deployment, exactly where novel compounds are under-covered. We introduced ADCC-CP, which calibrates over a class of applicability-domain shifts and so produces intervals that are provably valid — in finite samples and without estimating any density ratio — marginally, locally at every similarity level, and under every AD-aligned deployment shift, with a bound on what non-aligned shift can cost. Across thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets under temporal shift it restores conditional coverage better than stratified and estimated-weight conformal, matching the strongest estimate-based method while carrying the guarantee that method lacks, and it yields an interpretable, library-scale applicability-domain calibration curve. The approach gives practitioners not a heuristic but a certified, inspectable reliability schedule — and a label-free check of when it is relevant — to support safer decisions in computational drug discovery.
