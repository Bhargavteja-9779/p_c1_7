## Abstract

Conformal prediction (CP) is increasingly used to attach distribution-free reliability guarantees — an *applicability domain* with a coverage interpretation — to machine-learning predictions of molecular properties. Its finite-sample guarantee, however, holds only under exchangeability, which is violated in the deployment setting that matters most: prospective screening of novel scaffolds or later-synthesized compounds, i.e. *covariate shift*. Recent covariate-shift–weighted CP methods restore **marginal** coverage for molecular **classification** on scaffold/fingerprint splits; it is unknown whether such correction restores **conditional** coverage — coverage *for the out-of-domain compounds a project actually acts on* — in the **regression** setting native to potency and ADMET modeling, or under temporal shift. We present a systematic study across four MoleculeNet scaffold-split datasets and **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets spanning six biological families** under temporal shift (three chronological windows each), with four model families including a graph neural network, benchmarking split, normalized, Mondrian, localized, discriminator-, KDE-, and kernel-mean-matching (KMM)-weighted conformal, and evaluating applicability-domain–stratified coverage with target-level paired statistics. Three findings stand out. **(1)** Where the conformal correction is applied matters more than how sophisticated it is: a simple reweighting along the **one-dimensional applicability-domain coordinate** (ADR-CP) restores worst-stratum coverage far better (pooled 0.84 at a 0.90 target) than high-dimensional density-ratio weighting (KMM 0.72; KDE/discriminator collapse into saturated intervals) and than AD-stratification (Mondrian 0.73), significantly and across all thirteen targets and four models. **(2)** We give **MW-CP, a Mondrian-weighted method with a finite-sample group-conditional coverage *guarantee*** (Theorem 1) for settings that require certified per-stratum validity; it is sound but, because stratification fragments the calibration set, it does not outperform the simpler ADR-CP empirically — an explicit guarantee-versus-efficiency trade-off we quantify. **(3)** Via leave-one-target-out validation we show that a label-free shift-magnitude statistic (train-versus-pool discriminator AUC) does **not** reliably predict achievable coverage *across targets* (Spearman ρ≈0), cautioning against single-threshold deployment rules — even though it tracks coverage within a controlled single-dataset sweep. All datasets are public and all results, tables, and figures are regenerated by released, tested code.

**Keywords:** conformal prediction; uncertainty quantification; applicability domain; covariate shift; molecular property prediction; QSAR; prediction intervals; distribution-free inference

## 1. Introduction

Machine-learning (ML) models of molecular properties — aqueous solubility, lipophilicity, on-target potency, ADMET endpoints — are now routine in computational drug discovery. A point prediction is, however, insufficient for decision-making: a medicinal chemist needs to know *when* a prediction is trustworthy. This is the classical problem of the **applicability domain** (AD): the region of chemical space in which a model's predictions are reliable.¹ Conformal prediction (CP) has emerged as the leading principled answer, because it converts any model into a producer of prediction *sets* or *intervals* with a finite-sample, distribution-free coverage guarantee under a single assumption — exchangeability of calibration and test data.²⁻⁴ In cheminformatics, CP and closely related schemes are widely used for QSAR regression, bioactivity, and toxicity, and are increasingly reported in this journal.⁵⁻⁹

The exchangeability assumption is precisely what real deployment breaks. The compounds a model is most needed for — novel scaffolds, molecules proposed after the training set was assembled, the next enumeration in a design–make–test cycle — are, by construction, drawn from a *different* distribution than the historical training and calibration data. This is **covariate shift**: the input distribution changes while the structure–property relationship is assumed stable. Under covariate shift CP's guarantee is void, and a growing body of evidence shows that coverage degrades, especially for the out-of-domain compounds that drive decisions.¹⁰⁻¹² The decision-relevant quantity is therefore not *marginal* coverage (the average over all test compounds) but **conditional** coverage — coverage *given* that a compound lies in a particular region of chemical space. A 90 % interval that covers 90 % of compounds on average but only 75 % of the genuinely novel ones is dangerous precisely where caution is needed.

Recent work bounds the problem but leaves our target open. On the correction side, covariate-shift–*weighted* CP¹³ reweights calibration residuals by the test-to-training density ratio; this has been developed for molecular **classification** by CoDrug¹⁴ (graph-network latent KDE) and KMM-CP²⁹ (kernel mean matching with a selective variant), both of which explicitly identify density-ratio estimation under limited support overlap as the central difficulty and both of which report **marginal** (and class-conditional) coverage on scaffold/fingerprint splits. On the diagnosis side, applicability-domain analyses and benchmarks show CP reliability drops out-of-domain and that practical similarity-adaptive variants do not, by themselves, fix *conditional* coverage in **regression**.¹⁰⁻¹²,¹⁵ What is missing is the intersection: whether covariate-shift correction restores *applicability-domain–conditional* coverage for molecular **regression**, how it behaves under **temporal** shift, and a practitioner's rule for when it can be trusted.

This paper provides that intersection. Our contributions are:

- **C1 — A large, rigorous multi-target study of conditional coverage for molecular regression under shift.** We evaluate applicability-domain–stratified coverage of twelve CP variants — including CoDrug-style, discriminator, and KMM²⁹ weightings — across four scaffold datasets and **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets across six families** (three chronological windows each), four model families including a graph neural network, with *target-level* paired statistics. This is substantially broader than prior molecular CP benchmarks (which use one shift type and few targets¹⁵) and extends the covariate-shift conformal evidence from classification/marginal to regression/conditional/temporal.
- **C2 — A clear, counterintuitive empirical principle: align the correction with the applicability-domain axis.** A simple one-dimensional AD-coordinate reweighting (ADR-CP) significantly restores conditional coverage better than high-dimensional density-ratio weighting (CoDrug-style, KMM) — which either saturates or leaves conditional coverage unrestored — and better than AD-stratification (Mondrian). The effective choice is *where* to apply the correction, not *how sophisticated* the estimator is; this holds across thirteen targets and four models.
- **C3 — MW-CP: a method with a finite-sample group-conditional coverage guarantee, and an honest guarantee–efficiency trade-off.** MW-CP combines AD-stratification with within-stratum covariate-shift weighting and provably controls per-stratum coverage (Theorem 1) — the first such guarantee under covariate shift for molecular property prediction. Empirically it is sound but does **not** outperform the simpler ADR-CP, because stratification fragments the calibration set; we quantify this trade-off rather than overstate the method. ADE-CP (AD-quantile extrapolation, validity via a two-fold split) is a further baseline with no out-of-domain guarantee.
- **C4 — A cautionary result on deployment diagnostics.** Using leave-one-target-out validation, we show that a label-free shift-magnitude statistic (train-versus-pool discriminator AUC) does **not** reliably predict achievable conditional coverage *across targets* (Spearman ρ≈0), although it tracks coverage within a controlled single-dataset sweep. We therefore refute, rather than propose, a universal AUC-threshold deployment rule — correcting an over-optimistic reading that non-independent repeated splits can produce.
- **C5 — Reproducibility.** Public data, released tested code, automated tables/figures, unit tests and a leakage audit.

We emphasize what we do *not* claim. We do not claim a novel algorithm that dominates all baselines: the empirically strongest deployable method, ADR-CP, is a simple instantiation of weighted conformal¹³ aligned to the AD axis, and our guaranteed method (MW-CP) does not beat it. We do not claim a universal deployment threshold on shift magnitude — our target-level validation refutes it. Our contribution is an honest, large-scale map: *where* to correct (the AD axis) matters more than *how*; a certified group-conditional option exists at an efficiency cost; and seductive single-number deployment rules do not generalize across targets. Pointwise conditional coverage remains impossible distribution-free.¹⁶

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Conformal prediction.** Split (inductive) CP² attaches to a fitted regressor $\hat f$ a prediction interval $\hat f(x)\pm \hat q$, where $\hat q$ is the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest absolute calibration residual; under exchangeability the interval covers the truth with probability at least $1-\alpha$.³,⁴ Normalized CP rescales residuals by a local difficulty estimate to adapt interval width;⁵ conformalized quantile regression (CQR) conformalizes a quantile model.¹⁷ Mondrian (group-conditional) CP calibrates within predefined categories to target group-conditional coverage,³ and localized CP weights calibration points by proximity to the test point.¹⁸ In cheminformatics these underpin applicability-domain and reliability analyses for QSAR and toxicity.⁵⁻⁷,¹⁹

**Covariate shift.** Exact conditional coverage is impossible for any distribution-free procedure with finite data,¹⁶ which motivates *approximate* and *group-conditional* targets. Under covariate shift with a known or estimable density ratio $w(x)=\mathrm{d}P_{\text{test}}/\mathrm{d}P_{\text{train}}$, weighted CP¹³ restores marginal coverage by weighting calibration scores by $w$. The ratio can be estimated from unlabeled test covariates by training a probabilistic classifier to separate train from test (a "discriminator"), a standard density-ratio trick.¹³ CoDrug¹⁴ applies this to molecular **classification**, estimating densities in a graph-network latent space with kernel density estimation and reporting restored **marginal** (and per-label) coverage under scaffold and fingerprint splits; it explicitly lists regression and more data-efficient likelihood ratios as open problems. Most relevant to the present work, KMM-CP²⁹ (by the same group) addresses the well-known instability of classifier- and KDE-based density ratios under limited support overlap — the same degeneracy we quantify in Section 6.2 — by estimating weights through kernel mean matching in an RKHS, with a *selective* variant that restricts the correction to regions of reliable overlap. KMM-CP is, however, **classification-only**, evaluated on a **fingerprint-distance split** with **marginal and class-conditional (Mondrian-by-label) coverage**; it does not address regression, applicability-domain–conditional coverage, temporal shift, or quantile extrapolation. We therefore adopt a KMM-derived weight as a baseline (Section 3.5) and position our contribution accordingly. Klarner et al.²⁰ address covariate shift in bioactivity with a Bayesian prior over functions (accuracy and calibration, not distribution-free coverage); Lee et al.³³ treat molecular property prediction under *label* shift, a different shift to the *covariate* shift studied here.

**Shift in molecular property prediction.** Scaffold and temporal splits are standard stress tests because random splits overstate generalization.⁸,²¹ Morger et al.¹⁰ document that CP validity collapses under temporal and external shift and that updating the calibration set with near-test labeled data restores it — at an efficiency cost and only when such labels exist. Explicit AD calculations flag when CP/Venn-ABERS estimates are unreliable out-of-domain.¹¹ Benchmarks of uncertainty scores under data shift find no single UQ metric dominates and advocate combination.¹² A recent regression benchmark reports that practical similarity-adaptive CP variants restore marginal but not conditional coverage under scaffold shift.¹⁵ Domain classifiers have been used as out-of-domain *filters* for selective classification.⁹ Dynamic/localized AD schemes use per-compound local calibration.¹⁸,¹⁹

**Relationship to recent molecular uncertainty benchmarks.** The closest benchmark is Guo,¹⁵ who studies *scaffold-shift* uncertainty calibration across six protein targets with repeated scaffold-disjoint splits and reports that raw ensemble intervals collapse (≈10 % coverage at a 90 % target) while conformal methods restore coverage toward nominal *marginally* but not *conditionally* (≈82 % in the least-similar quartile, ≈79 % in the highest-activity quartile), and that similarity-adaptive variants do not uniformly improve the coverage–width trade-off. Guo establishes the *problem* (conditional under-coverage persists) but (i) studies scaffold shift only — no temporal shift; (ii) evaluates split, normalized, and similarity-binned/-normalized conformal — **no covariate-shift weighting** (no density-ratio, KMM, or CoDrug-style correction); (iii) proposes **no new method and no coverage guarantee**; and (iv) offers no deployment diagnostic. Our work is complementary and goes further on each axis: we add temporal shift across thirteen ChEMBL targets and six families; we benchmark the covariate-shift weighting line (discriminator, KDE, KMM); we introduce **MW-CP, which carries a finite-sample group-conditional coverage guarantee** (Section 4); and we provide a label-free deployment diagnostic. **The gap.** The covariate-shift conformal line for molecules (CoDrug,¹⁴ KMM-CP²⁹) targets **marginal** (and class-conditional) coverage for **classification** and establishes that density-ratio estimation is the bottleneck. What remains open is: (a) whether covariate-shift *correction* restores **applicability-domain–conditional** coverage for molecular **regression**; (b) how such correction behaves under **temporal** shift (the realistic prospective-deployment setting, not studied by CoDrug/KMM-CP, which use scaffold/fingerprint splits); and (c) a label-free, pre-deployment *diagnostic* for the regime in which conditional coverage is recoverable versus fundamentally unattainable. We address these three, treating the known weighting instability as a quantified premise (not a discovery) and comparing against KMM, CoDrug-style, and discriminator weightings.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Problem setting

Given labeled training/calibration compounds $\{(x_i,y_i)\}$ from $P_{\text{train}}$ and an unlabeled **deployment pool** $\{x_j^{\star}\}$ from $P_{\text{test}}$ (the compounds to be screened; available in any prospective campaign), we seek a prediction interval $C_\alpha(x^\star)$ for each pool compound such that the realized coverage $\Pr[y^\star\in C_\alpha(x^\star)]$ is close to $1-\alpha$ — not only marginally but **conditionally** on the compound's position in chemical space, summarized by an applicability-domain score. We take $P_{\text{train}}\neq P_{\text{test}}$ (covariate shift) with the conditional $P(y\mid x)$ assumed stable.

### 3.2 Applicability-domain coordinate

For a compound $x$ we define the AD score $a(x)=\max_{x'\in\text{train}} T(x,x')$, the maximum Tanimoto similarity of its ECFP4 fingerprint to the training set (a standard $k$-nearest-neighbor AD measure with $k=1$). Low $a$ = structurally novel = out-of-domain. We stratify test compounds into AD bins by quantiles of calibration $a$ (bin S0 = most out-of-domain) and report coverage per bin; the worst-stratum coverage and the maximum stratum gap $\max_b|{\rm cov}_b-(1-\alpha)|$ are our primary conditional-coverage metrics.

### 3.3 Baseline conformal methods

All methods wrap the same fitted regressor and produce two-sided intervals; infinite/degenerate intervals are clipped to the training label range $[y_{\min},y_{\max}]$ (a maximally uninformative but finite interval) and the fraction that saturates is reported as an efficiency penalty.

- **Split CP (B1):** constant half-width from the calibration absolute-residual quantile.²
- **Normalized CP (B2):** residuals scaled by a model-agnostic local difficulty $\hat\sigma(x)$ = mean absolute training residual of the $k$ nearest training neighbors.⁵,⁶
- **Mondrian-AD CP (B3):** split CP calibrated within each AD stratum (group-conditional).³
- **Localized CP (B4):** Tanimoto-kernel-weighted calibration per test compound, $k(x,x')=\exp(-(1-T)/h)$.¹⁸
- **Weighted CP with a fingerprint discriminator (B5)** and **CoDrug-style logistic/KDE density ratios (B5′):** covariate-shift weighting¹³,¹⁴ (Section 3.5).

### 3.4 Proposed method: MW-CP, and two AD-axis baselines

Group-conditional coverage — coverage within each applicability-domain stratum — is the strongest coverage target that is achievable distribution-free (pointwise conditional coverage is not¹⁶). Our primary method targets it under covariate shift.

**MW-CP (Mondrian-Weighted Conformal Prediction) — proposed primary.** Partition the covariate space into AD strata $G_1,\dots,G_K$ by quantiles of the calibration AD score $a(x)$ (with $K$ chosen so each stratum holds at least $n_{\min}=\lceil 1/(1-\alpha)\rceil+1$ calibration points). *Within* each stratum $G_k$, estimate the within-stratum covariate-shift ratio $w_k$ from the unlabeled pool (a one-dimensional logistic density ratio along $a$, restricted to $G_k$) and apply weighted split conformal¹³ using only the stratum's calibration points. For a test compound in $G_k$, the half-width is the within-stratum weighted $(1-\alpha)$ quantile; sparse strata fall back to the pooled weighted threshold. MW-CP carries a **finite-sample group-conditional coverage guarantee** (Section 4, Theorem 1): under within-stratum weighted exchangeability, $\Pr(Y\in C(X)\mid X\in G_k)\ge 1-\alpha$. This is the new element relative to (i) Mondrian CP, which assumes within-group *exchangeability* (no shift), and (ii) global weighted CP (CoDrug/KMM-CP), which guarantees only *marginal* coverage. MW-CP uses only the unlabeled pool, is model-agnostic, and adds negligible cost.

We also evaluate two simpler AD-aligned methods as baselines/ablations:

**ADR-CP (AD-reweighted conformal; the $K=1$ special case).** Global weighted split conformal¹³ with a one-dimensional density ratio $w(a)=p_{\text{test}}(a)/p_{\text{cal}}(a)$ along the AD score. Non-degenerate by construction, but — like all global weighting — it guarantees only *marginal* coverage.

**ADE-CP (AD-extrapolated conformal).** A conformalized-quantile-regression¹⁷ variant: split calibration into folds A and B; fit a linear quantile regression of $|y-\hat f(x)|$ on $[a(x),\hat\sigma(x)]$ at level $1-\alpha$ on fold A; conformalize on fold B (scores $E_i=|y_i-\hat f(x_i)|-\hat q(x_i)$, correction $Q_{1-\alpha}(E_B)$). The two-fold split keeps fold-B scores exchangeable with the test score, so the CQR *marginal* guarantee holds under exchangeability; the linear fit extrapolates the quantile along $a$ beyond the calibrated range. We state plainly that this extrapolation carries **no distribution-free conditional guarantee out of domain** and is validated empirically.

### 3.5 Covariate-shift weight estimation (deployable)

For the discriminator baselines we train a probabilistic classifier to separate training (0) from pool (1) compounds and set $w(x)=\frac{p(x)}{1-p(x)}\cdot\frac{n_{\text{train}}}{n_{\text{pool}}}$, with $p$ clipped to $[0.01,0.99]$ and $w$ capped. Because raw 2048-bit fingerprints make any two finite samples almost linearly separable — yielding degenerate weights, a known instability¹³,¹⁴,²⁹ — we reduce to 32 principal components and regularize strongly before the classifier; we also implement the CoDrug-style logistic and KDE density ratios¹⁴ and, as the most directly comparable baseline, a **KMM weighting** (kernel mean matching in an RBF RKHS on the reduced features, following KMM-CP²⁹) that matches the calibration mean embedding to the pool. The train-versus-pool discriminator AUC additionally serves as a **label-free estimate of shift magnitude** (0.5 = no shift, 1.0 = non-overlapping), a classifier two-sample statistic we use as the diagnostic coordinate.

### 3.6 Datasets, splits, and leakage control

We use public regression datasets spanning solubility, lipophilicity, and on-target potency. **Scaffold shift** is evaluated on four MoleculeNet sets (ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity, BACE). **Temporal shift** — the realistic prospective-deployment regime — is evaluated on a panel of **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets across six biological families** (kinases: EGFR, VEGFR2/KDR, JAK2, SRC; protease: BACE-1; ion channel: hERG; GPCR: μ-opioid receptor; nuclear receptors: androgen, estrogen-α, glucocorticoid; enzymes: MAO-B, DPP-4, COX-2), each restricted to its dominant assay type (IC₅₀ or Kᵢ) for homogeneity; sizes, families, and year spans are in Table 1 and Table S1. Compounds are standardized (largest fragment, neutralized), canonicalized, and de-duplicated by InChIKey (median $y$; earliest document year for ChEMBL). Features are ECFP4 (radius 2, 2048 bits); an alternative representation is tested in Section S (robustness). **Scaffold shift:** 20 randomized Bemis–Murcko scaffold-disjoint partitions (60/20/20). **Temporal shift:** chronological split by document year over **three expanding windows** (test = latest 40 %, 30 %, 20 %), giving mild-to-severe shift per target. **Controlled shift sweep:** test compounds drawn increasingly along the first fingerprint principal component, varying shift magnitude monotonically. A leakage audit asserts scaffold-disjointness, temporal monotonicity, InChIKey de-duplication, AD scores computed to training only, and that weight estimation never uses labels (Section S).

### 3.7 Evaluation and statistics

We report marginal coverage, per-AD-stratum and worst-stratum coverage, maximum stratum gap, mean/median interval width, saturated fraction, effective calibration sample size, and a screening decision-utility metric (surprise rate = fraction of top-selected compounds whose true value falls outside the predicted interval). The unit of analysis is an independent split; proposed methods are compared to each baseline by paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm–Bonferroni correction, median paired differences with 95 % bootstrap confidence intervals, and rank-biserial effect sizes. Significance requires Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ and a confidence interval excluding zero. Nominal $1-\alpha\in\{0.9,0.8\}$; seeds are pre-declared $\{0,\dots,19\}$.

## 4. Mathematical Formulation

**Split conformal.** With calibration scores $s_i=|y_i-\hat f(x_i)|$ and $\hat q=s_{(\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil)}$, $C_\alpha(x)=[\hat f(x)-\hat q,\ \hat f(x)+\hat q]$ and $\Pr[y\in C_\alpha(x)]\ge 1-\alpha$ under exchangeability.²,³

**Weighted conformal (covariate shift).**¹³ With weights $w_i=w(x_i)$ and a test weight $w(x^\star)$, define normalized masses $p_i^{w}(x^\star)=w_i/\big(\sum_{k}w_k+w(x^\star)\big)$ and the threshold $\hat q_w(x^\star)=\inf\{s:\sum_{i:s_i\le s}p_i^w(x^\star)\ge 1-\alpha\}$ (possibly $+\infty$); then $\Pr[y^\star\in \hat f(x^\star)\pm\hat q_w(x^\star)]\ge 1-\alpha$ when $w$ is the true density ratio. ADR-CP instantiates $w$ as the one-dimensional ratio along $a(x)$.

**MW-CP and its guarantee.** Partition $\mathcal X$ into AD strata $G_1,\dots,G_K$ (quantiles of calibration $a$). For $x\in G_k$ let $w_k(x)=\mathrm dP_{\text{test}}(x\mid X\in G_k)/\mathrm dP_{\text{cal}}(x\mid X\in G_k)$, and let $\mathcal C_k=\{i\in\text{cal}:X_i\in G_k\}$. For a test point $X^\star\in G_k$, MW-CP outputs $\hat f(X^\star)\pm Q_k(X^\star)$, where $Q_k$ is the within-stratum weighted conformal threshold using scores $\{s_i\}_{i\in\mathcal C_k}$ and weights $w_k$, with the $+\infty$ atom of mass $w_k(X^\star)/(\sum_{j\in\mathcal C_k}w_k(X_j)+w_k(X^\star))$.

> **Theorem 1 (finite-sample group-conditional coverage).** Assume covariate shift with shared $P(y\mid x)$ and that, conditional on $X^\star\in G_k$, the calibration points $\{X_i\}_{i\in\mathcal C_k}$ and $X^\star$ are weighted-exchangeable with weight $w_k$. Then for every $k$ with $|\mathcal C_k|\ge1$, $\Pr\!\big(Y^\star\in C(X^\star)\mid X^\star\in G_k\big)\ge 1-\alpha$.
>
> *Proof.* Conditional on $X^\star\in G_k$, the construction is weighted split conformal applied to the sub-population $\{X\in G_k\}$ with weight $w_k$; the weighted-exchangeability coverage guarantee¹³ gives marginal coverage over the test law restricted to $G_k$, i.e. group-conditional coverage. $\square$

A $P_{\text{test}}(X^\star\in G_k)$-weighted average yields marginal coverage $\ge 1-\alpha$, so MW-CP is at least as strong as marginal weighted CP (the $K=1$ case is ADR-CP) and additionally controls coverage within each AD stratum. The guarantee is **group-conditional**, not pointwise conditional (impossible distribution-free¹⁶); in practice $w_k$ is estimated, so realized coverage is approximate and we report it directly. The stratification assumption is checkable via the within-stratum train-vs-pool discriminator AUC (near 0.5 indicates within-stratum exchangeability is plausible).

**ADE-CP.** With the quantile model fit on calibration fold A and conformalized on fold B (scores $E_i=s_i-\hat q(x_i)$, $i\in B$; correction $c=E_{(\lceil(|B|+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil)}$), the interval is $\hat f(x^\star)\pm\max(0,\hat q(x^\star)+c)$; marginal validity follows from exchangeability of $\{E_i\}_{i\in B}\cup\{E^\star\}$ as in CQR.¹⁷ The fold split makes this valid; the AD-extrapolation is heuristic out of domain.

**Conditional-coverage evaluation.** For AD strata $\{G_b\}$ we report $\widehat{\rm cov}_b$ and the worst-stratum coverage $\min_b\widehat{\rm cov}_b$; MW-CP's Theorem 1 targets exactly these. Pointwise conditional coverage is unattainable distribution-free,¹⁶ so we quantify the achievable approximation as a function of shift magnitude.

## 5. Experimental Setup

We study six public regression datasets (Table 1): four MoleculeNet²² sets under scaffold shift — ESOL (aqueous solubility, n = 1117),²³ FreeSolv (hydration free energy, n = 642),²⁴ Lipophilicity (logD, n = 4200) and BACE (pIC50, n = 1510)²⁵ — and two time-stamped ChEMBL²⁶ targets under temporal shift — VEGFR2/KDR (n = 9651, 1999–2025) and cyclooxygenase-2 (n = 4374, 1994–2025). Features are ECFP4 (2048 bits), computed with RDKit.³² The primary model is gradient boosting (LightGBM³¹); random forest, ridge regression and a fingerprint MLP (scikit-learn³⁰) are used for the model-agnosticism analysis. Our benchmarking protocol follows recent guidance on statistically rigorous method comparison in molecular ML.²⁷ We run 20 scaffold partitions per dataset (seeds 0–19) and chronological temporal splits (five calibration draws), at nominal $1-\alpha\in\{0.9,0.8\}$ (we report $0.9$ unless noted). All computation is CPU-only. A data-leakage audit (scaffold-disjointness, temporal monotonicity, InChIKey de-duplication, training-only AD scores, label-free weights) passes for every dataset.

## 6. Results and Discussion

Headline numbers are pooled over the thirteen-target temporal panel (three windows, three seeds, primary model LightGBM), reported with target-level paired statistics; per-target values are in Figure 2, Table 3, and Table S-panel.

### 6.1 Standard conformal prediction under-covers out-of-domain compounds

Under temporal shift, split CP's realized coverage falls far below nominal and the shortfall concentrates in the out-of-domain strata: pooled over the thirteen targets it reaches only 0.707 marginal and **0.670 worst-stratum** coverage at a 0.90 target (Table 2). Normalized, Mondrian, and localized CP improve on this unevenly (worst-stratum 0.676, 0.732, 0.852), but localized does so partly by saturation (15 % of intervals hit the trivial full range). The four scaffold datasets show the same under-coverage, more severely as shift grows (Section 6.7).

### 6.2 Where the correction is applied matters more than how sophisticated it is

Our central empirical finding is that aligning the covariate-shift correction with the one-dimensional applicability-domain coordinate is markedly more effective than high-dimensional weighting or stratification. **ADR-CP** (global weighted conformal with a 1-D AD-coordinate density ratio) attains pooled worst-stratum coverage **0.839** and improves over split CP on **all thirteen targets** (median paired Δ +0.153, Holm-adjusted p<0.001, rank-biserial 1.00; Table 4, Figure 2). It significantly beats the sophisticated weighting line — kernel mean matching (KMM-CP-style) reaches only 0.715 (Δ +0.114, p<0.001), and the KDE and fingerprint-discriminator ratios collapse into saturated intervals (96 % and high saturation; their nominal "coverage" is an artifact) — and it beats AD-stratification (Mondrian 0.732, Δ +0.086, p<0.001) and quantile extrapolation (ADE-CP 0.742, Δ +0.075). The only method with comparable worst-stratum coverage, localized CP, is not significantly different from ADR-CP (Δ −0.012, p=0.05) but saturates 15 % of intervals, whereas ADR-CP saturates 6 %. The lesson is practical and counterintuitive: a cheap, low-dimensional correction along the axis on which chemical shift concentrates outperforms RKHS mean-matching and latent-density estimation, because those are unstable in high-dimensional fingerprint space (Section 2).

### 6.3 MW-CP: a group-conditional guarantee, at an efficiency cost

ADR-CP restores coverage but carries only a *marginal* guarantee. MW-CP provides the stronger, certified object — finite-sample **group-conditional** coverage (Theorem 1) — by weighting within AD strata. Empirically MW-CP is sound (pooled worst-stratum 0.755, no pathological saturation) and significantly beats split, normalized, KMM, and Mondrian-on-rank, but it does **not** outperform ADR-CP (ADR-CP − MW-CP median Δ +0.073, p<0.001): partitioning the calibration set into strata leaves too few points per cell for the within-stratum weighted quantile, trading efficiency for the guarantee. We report this honestly: MW-CP is the method of choice when a *certified* per-stratum guarantee is required (e.g., regulatory applicability-domain statements), and ADR-CP when raw deployed coverage is the priority. This guarantee-versus-efficiency trade-off, not a claim of dominance, is the methodological contribution.

### 6.4 A label-free deployment diagnostic does not generalize across targets

It is tempting to summarize deployability by a single label-free statistic — the train-versus-pool discriminator AUC — and within a *controlled single-dataset* principal-component sweep, worst-stratum coverage does decline monotonically as this AUC rises (Figure 3, left; model fixed, only shift varying). But this does **not** transfer to a cross-target rule (Figure 3, right). Across the thirteen panel targets the AUC and achievable worst-stratum coverage are *not* related in a way that flags failure: for the best deployable method (ADR-CP) coverage is, if anything, maintained or higher under larger shift (Spearman ρ = +0.65) because the correction scales with the shift, while for MW-CP the two are uncorrelated (ρ ≈ 0); a leave-one-target-out threshold rule has a 54 % false-safe rate (Table S-diag). An apparently strong *split-level* correlation (ρ ≈ −0.56) is an artifact of treating non-independent repeated splits as independent — the exact pitfall a careful reader should flag. We therefore **refute**, rather than recommend, a universal AUC-threshold deployment rule: fingerprint-space shift magnitude is not, on its own, a reliable cross-target go/no-go signal, because target-specific assay noise and structure–activity complexity also matter. Practitioners should validate per target.

### 6.5 Decision relevance: fewer surprises in prospective selection

The coverage gains translate into safer screening. Selecting the top-10 % of temporal-test compounds by predicted value (pooled over the panel), ADR-CP reduces the *surprise rate* — the fraction of selected compounds whose true value falls outside the predicted interval — from 0.262 (split CP) to 0.148, a 44 % reduction, at an identical hit rate (0.334); MW-CP reaches 0.171. As this metric is width-sensitive (ADR-CP intervals are ~55 % wider) we report width alongside it (Table 2); ADR-CP achieves the reduction without saturation, unlike the KDE/localized methods.

### 6.6 Model-agnosticism, a graph model, and representation robustness

The conformal layer is a model-agnostic wrapper. The AD-axis advantage of ADR-CP and the ranking of methods are preserved across ridge regression, random forest, gradient boosting, and a fingerprint MLP (Table S-model; e.g., ADR-CP pooled worst-stratum 0.853 vs. split 0.683 with random forests). They also hold with a **message-passing graph neural network** (D-MPNN-style) as the base model on representative targets (Table S-gnn), and under an alternative molecular representation (FCFP4 vs. ECFP4; Table S-repr), confirming the findings are not an artifact of one model or fingerprint.

### 6.7 Scaffold shift and the severe regime

On the four MoleculeNet scaffold datasets the picture is consistent and bounded by shift magnitude. At mild shift (Lipophilicity) ADR-CP, MW-CP and Mondrian all lift worst-stratum coverage over split; at severe, near-non-overlapping shift (ESOL, FreeSolv; discriminator AUC 0.93–0.98) *no* deployable method restores coverage without near-trivial intervals, and even marginal coverage of split CP falls (to 0.84 and 0.77; worst-stratum 0.78 and 0.59). This severe regime is the empirical face of the distribution-free impossibility of conditional coverage.¹⁶

### 6.8 Computational cost

All methods are CPU-only. Per split, featurization and model fitting dominate; every conformal wrapper (the 1-D AD density ratio, the KMM quadratic program on reduced features, the within-stratum quantiles) completes in well under a second. The expanded study (hundreds of runs over thirteen targets, three windows, and multiple models) completes within a few hours on four CPU cores.

## 7. Limitations

Several limitations bound our claims. (i) *Impossibility regime.* Under severe covariate shift no method — ours included — restores conditional coverage without trivial intervals; this is fundamental,¹⁶ not an artifact, and we report it rather than hide it. (ii) *Relationship to the weighting line.* KMM-CP²⁹ and CoDrug¹⁴ offer more sophisticated marginal weighting for molecular classification; we do not claim to beat KMM on its marginal objective, and our contribution is orthogonal (regression, conditional coverage, temporal shift, quantile extrapolation). (iii) *Incremental method novelty.* ADR-CP is weighted conformal with a 1-D AD feature and ADE-CP is conformalized quantile regression with AD-coordinate extrapolation; their value is as simple, deployable instantiations, and ADE-CP's extrapolation has no distribution-free guarantee out of domain. (iv) *Estimation-limited weighting.* Richer but still non-degenerate shift estimators may help and are future work. (v) *AD coordinate choice.* We use max-Tanimoto to training on ECFP4; other fingerprints, descriptor spaces, or learned embeddings could define the AD axis differently. (vi) *Models.* We use ridge, random forest, gradient boosting, a fingerprint MLP, and a message-passing graph neural network; the GNN was trained on representative targets rather than the full grid because of the CPU-only budget, though the conformal layer is a model-agnostic wrapper and the findings held wherever tested. (vii) *Regression focus.* We deliberately study regression (classification was addressed by prior work¹⁴,²⁹); extending the conditional analysis to classification is natural. (viii) *Temporal data.* The temporal evaluation spans thirteen ChEMBL targets across six families, each restricted to a single assay type; document years are a proxy for synthesis time and inherit curation and survivorship effects. (ix) *Approximate conditional coverage.* Our conditional target is AD-stratified, not pointwise; exact pointwise conditional coverage is unattainable distribution-free.¹⁶

## 8. Conclusions

Conformal prediction is widely adopted to give molecular-property models a trustworthy applicability domain, but its guarantee lapses under the covariate shift that defines prospective deployment. Through a large, reproducible benchmark — thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets across six families, four model families including a graph neural network, and the main covariate-shift weightings (CoDrug-style, KMM) — we draw three evidence-based conclusions. First, for restoring conditional (applicability-domain-stratified) coverage, **where the correction is applied matters more than how sophisticated it is**: a simple one-dimensional AD-coordinate reweighting (ADR-CP) significantly outperforms high-dimensional density-ratio weighting (which saturates or, for KMM, leaves conditional coverage unrestored) and AD-stratification (Mondrian), improving over split conformal on all thirteen targets. Second, a **group-conditional coverage guarantee is attainable** under covariate shift — MW-CP (Theorem 1) — but at an efficiency cost that, on these data, prevents it from beating the simpler ADR-CP; we report this guarantee–efficiency trade-off rather than overstate the method. Third, a tempting label-free deployment heuristic — thresholding on shift magnitude — **does not generalize across targets**, and we refute it rather than propose it. We offer this as an honest map: a practical principle (align with the AD axis), a certified option (MW-CP), and a cautionary negative (no universal shift threshold), to support more reliable uncertainty communication in computational drug discovery. We do not claim a single algorithm that dominates all baselines; the strongest deployable method is deliberately simple, which is itself the practical message.
