We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"Applicability-Domain–Aware Conformal Prediction for Molecular Property Regression under Covariate Shift: When Correction Helps, When It Cannot, and a Deployment Diagnostic,"** for consideration as an Article in the *Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling*.

**Problem and motivation.** Conformal prediction is now a leading way to attach a distribution-free applicability-domain statement to machine-learning predictions of molecular properties, and it appears frequently in this journal. Its coverage guarantee, however, assumes exchangeability — precisely the assumption that fails in prospective deployment, where the compounds to be scored are novel scaffolds or later-synthesized molecules (covariate shift). Recent JCIM work shows that uncertainty estimates become unreliable out-of-domain and that no single remedy dominates; a NeurIPS 2023 method restores *marginal* coverage for molecular *classification* via density-ratio weighting. What the field lacks — and what decisions actually depend on — is whether such correction restores **conditional** coverage (coverage for the out-of-domain compounds one acts on) in the **regression** setting native to potency and ADMET, together with a method that is deployable from only the unlabeled screening pool, and a rule for when any of this can be trusted.

**What we contribute.** Across [[N_DATASETS]] public datasets (MoleculeNet and time-stamped ChEMBL targets), four model families, and both scaffold and temporal shift, with repeated-split paired statistics, we show: (1) standard and localized conformal methods substantially under-cover out-of-domain compounds; (2) naively porting covariate-shift weighting to regression fails because high-dimensional fingerprint and KDE density-ratio estimators are degenerate — a cautionary result we trace to *estimation*, not to weighted conformal itself; (3) aligning the correction with the applicability-domain axis — by reweighting (ADR-CP) or by extrapolating the conformal residual quantile (ADE-CP) — restores coverage robustly and deployably, most clearly under realistic temporal shift; and (4) the achievable conditional coverage is governed by a label-free shift-magnitude diagnostic (a train-versus-pool discriminator AUC), which yields a practical decision rule. We are candid about the boundary: under severe, near-non-overlapping shift no deployable method restores conditional coverage without near-trivial intervals, consistent with the known impossibility of exact distribution-free conditional validity. We do not overclaim a universal solution.
