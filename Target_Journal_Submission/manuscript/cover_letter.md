We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"Applicability-Domain–Aware Conformal Prediction for Molecular Property Regression under Covariate Shift: When Correction Helps, When It Cannot, and a Deployment Diagnostic,"** for consideration as an Article in the *Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling*.

**Problem and motivation.** Conformal prediction is now a leading way to attach a distribution-free applicability-domain statement to machine-learning predictions of molecular properties, and it appears frequently in this journal. Its coverage guarantee, however, assumes exchangeability — precisely the assumption that fails in prospective deployment, where the compounds to be scored are novel scaffolds or later-synthesized molecules (covariate shift). Recent JCIM work shows that uncertainty estimates become unreliable out-of-domain and that no single remedy dominates; a NeurIPS 2023 method restores *marginal* coverage for molecular *classification* via density-ratio weighting. What the field lacks — and what decisions actually depend on — is whether such correction restores **conditional** coverage (coverage for the out-of-domain compounds one acts on) in the **regression** setting native to potency and ADMET, together with a method that is deployable from only the unlabeled screening pool, and a rule for when any of this can be trusted.

**What we contribute.** Across four MoleculeNet scaffold datasets and **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets spanning six biological families** under temporal shift (three windows each), four model families including a graph neural network, and target-level paired statistics — benchmarking split, normalized, Mondrian, localized, discriminator-, KDE-, and kernel-mean-matching (KMM)-weighted conformal — we establish: (1) *where* the covariate-shift correction is applied matters more than *how sophisticated* it is — a simple one-dimensional applicability-domain reweighting (ADR-CP) significantly restores conditional (worst-stratum) coverage better than high-dimensional density-ratio weighting (KMM, KDE) and than AD-stratification (Mondrian), improving over split conformal on all thirteen targets; (2) MW-CP, a Mondrian-weighted method, carries a finite-sample **group-conditional coverage guarantee** (the first under covariate shift for molecular property prediction), and we honestly quantify its guarantee-versus-efficiency trade-off — it does not outperform the simpler ADR-CP; and (3) via leave-one-target-out validation, a label-free shift-magnitude statistic does **not** reliably predict achievable coverage across targets, so we *refute* a universal deployment-threshold rule rather than propose one. We are candid throughout: the empirically best method is simple, the guaranteed method trades efficiency for its guarantee, and no method restores coverage under severe near-non-overlapping shift. We position the work as a rigorous, reproducible benchmark and set of practical principles complementary to the marginal-coverage weighting line (CoDrug, KMM-CP), and we do not overclaim a dominant novel algorithm.

**Fit with JCIM.** The work is squarely within the journal's active stream on uncertainty quantification, applicability domain, benchmarking, and trustworthy QSAR. It adheres to the journal's reproducibility expectations: all datasets are public, and all results, tables, and figures are regenerated by released, tested code with a one-command entry point and a data-leakage audit.
