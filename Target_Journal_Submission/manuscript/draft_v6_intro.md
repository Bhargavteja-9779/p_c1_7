## 1. Introduction

Machine-learning models of potency, ADMET and physicochemical endpoints are routine in drug discovery, but a point prediction is only actionable when it comes with an honest statement of how wrong it may be. The classical answer is the **applicability domain** (AD): predictions are trusted near the training data and distrusted far from it.¹ Conformal prediction (CP) turned this intuition into a guarantee — any model can be wrapped to output intervals that contain the true value with a user-chosen probability, assuming only exchangeability of calibration and test compounds²⁻⁴ — and it is now widely used for QSAR and ADMET models, including in this journal.⁵⁻⁸

The guarantee fails exactly where it is needed. Prospective compounds — the next design cycle, a newly enumerated library, molecules made after the model was trained — are, by construction, *not* exchangeable with the historical calibration set: their distribution over chemical space has shifted (covariate shift). Conformal coverage then degrades, and it degrades unevenly: intervals remain roughly valid for familiar chemotypes and under-cover the structurally novel compounds that drive decisions.¹⁰⁻¹²,¹⁵,³⁷ A nominal 90 % interval that covers 70 % of the most novel compounds is most misleading precisely where a chemist needs caution.

Two families of fixes exist, and both have a structural weakness. **Stratified (Mondrian) conformal** calibrates separately within AD bins;³ it assumes no shift *within* a bin and fragments the calibration set, so the most novel bin — where test compounds are typically even more novel than any calibration compound — remains under-covered. **Weighted conformal**¹³ reweights calibration residuals by an *estimated* density ratio between deployment and calibration chemistry; for molecules this has been developed by CoDrug¹⁴ and KMM-CP,²⁹ both of which identify ratio estimation in high-dimensional fingerprint space as the central difficulty. Estimated ratios are unstable, collapse the effective calibration size, and — crucially — carry a guarantee only if the estimate is right. In our earlier large-scale benchmark, the most effective practical fix was a simple *one-dimensional* reweighting along the AD coordinate (ADR-CP), but it, too, is an estimate plugged in as weights, without a finite-sample guarantee.

Here we remove the estimation step altogether. We observe that the shifts that matter for molecular deployment are, to first order, shifts *along the applicability-domain axis* — a library or a later project is enriched for compounds of lower similarity to the training set, by an unknown amount. Recent conformal theory shows that one can guarantee coverage *simultaneously over an entire class of covariate shifts* by calibrating with an augmented quantile regression over that class.³⁴ We instantiate this idea for chemistry: **Applicability-Domain Conditional Conformal prediction (ADCC-CP)** calibrates over the class of all continuous piecewise-linear functions of the AD coordinate. The resulting intervals are valid, in finite samples and without any density-ratio estimate, (i) marginally, (ii) locally around every similarity level of the applicability domain, and (iii) under *every* deployment shift whose density ratio along the AD axis lies in the class. Because the interval width depends on a compound only through its AD value, ADCC-CP produces an interpretable **AD calibration curve** — interval width as a function of similarity to the training set — that can be applied to libraries of any size by lookup.

Our contributions are:

- **C1 — ADCC-CP, the first conformal method for molecular property prediction with a finite-sample coverage guarantee over a whole family of applicability-domain shifts that requires no density-ratio estimation** (Theorem 1), together with an exact, scalable algorithm (Algorithm 1) and a model-agnostic implementation.
- **C2 — A misspecification bound** (Proposition 2) that states how much coverage can be lost when the real shift is *not* AD-aligned — never more than for standard conformal, and less whenever part of the shift is AD-aligned — which explains when AD-based correction is sufficient and when it cannot be.
- **C3 — Rigorous, pre-registered evaluation.** The function class was selected on MoleculeNet scaffold splits only and then frozen; the held-out evaluation covers **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL targets from six protein families**, three chronological windows, two model families and three seeds, with target-level (n = 13) statistics. Exchangeable and known-shift controls verify the guarantee empirically.
- **C4 — Practical guidance.** We show where ADCC-CP improves on the strongest estimated-weight method and where no AD-based method can close the gap, and release code and data for full reproduction.
