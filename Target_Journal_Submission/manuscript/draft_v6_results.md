## 4. Experimental Design

### 4.1 Data, splits, models

**Scaffold shift (development only).** Four MoleculeNet²² regression sets — ESOL,²³ FreeSolv,²⁴ Lipophilicity, and BACE²⁵ — split into 20 Bemis–Murcko scaffold-disjoint partitions (60/20/20). These were used *only* to select the ADCC-CP function class (Section 4.2) and never enter the confirmatory evaluation.

**Temporal shift (held-out confirmatory).** A panel of **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL²⁶ targets across six biological families** — kinases (EGFR, VEGFR2/KDR, JAK2, SRC), a protease (BACE-1), an ion channel (hERG), a GPCR (μ-opioid receptor), nuclear receptors (androgen, estrogen-α, glucocorticoid) and enzymes (MAO-B, DPP-4, COX-2) — 2088–5163 molecules each, 1989–2025, each restricted to its dominant assay type (IC₅₀ or Kᵢ). Compounds are split chronologically by document year into three expanding windows (test = latest 40 %, 30 %, 20 %), giving mild-to-severe shift. Features are ECFP4 (2048 bits, RDKit³²). Base models are gradient boosting (LightGBM³¹) and random forest (scikit-learn³⁰); the conformal layer is a model-agnostic wrapper. Three calibration seeds per configuration. A leakage audit (chronological monotonicity, InChIKey de-duplication, training-only AD scores, label-free pool) passes for every target.

### 4.2 Pre-registration

The function class was chosen on the scaffold development data alone and frozen before any ChEMBL run (`supplementary/adcc_preregistration.md`). Among four candidate classes — indicator (Mondrian-equivalent), piecewise-linear, continuous spline, and normalized-score — the **continuous linear spline** ($d=5$) was selected for the best worst-stratum coverage at the lowest saturation and smallest dimension. All other classes are reported on the panel as pre-specified ablations. The primary endpoint is worst-AD-stratum coverage at $\alpha=0.10$; secondary endpoints are coverage in the most out-of-domain stratum (S0), coverage on the most-novel decile, interval width, and saturated fraction. Statistics are computed at the **target level** ($n=13$): paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Holm-corrected) and cluster bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals over targets, never treating the {{NRUNS}} runs as independent.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 The guarantee holds: validity controls

Before the shift experiments we verify ADCC-CP's finite-sample behaviour on controls where ground truth is known (Table {{T_validity}}). Under **exchangeable random splits** (no shift) every method is at nominal marginal coverage, and ADCC-CP is additionally at nominal coverage within every AD stratum (S0 coverage {{VAL_rand_s0_adcc}} at a 0.90 target), confirming it does not sacrifice validity for adaptivity. Under a **controlled shift with a known density ratio that is a piecewise-linear function of the AD coordinate** — inside ADCC-CP's class, so Theorem 1 applies exactly — ADCC-CP restores S0 coverage to {{VAL_plshift_s0_adcc}} whereas split conformal under-covers ({{VAL_plshift_s0_split}}). Under a known shift whose ratio is *exponential* in the AD coordinate — outside the class — ADCC-CP still corrects most of the shortfall, as Proposition 2 predicts. These controls demonstrate empirically what the theory asserts: coverage is maintained whenever the shift is AD-aligned, without any ratio being estimated.

### 5.2 Temporal shift: conditional coverage across thirteen targets

Under temporal shift, split conformal under-covers overall and severely in the out-of-domain strata: pooled over the panel it reaches only {{POOL_marg_split}} marginal and **{{POOL_wsc_split}} worst-stratum** coverage at a 0.90 target (Table {{T_pooled}}). Normalized and KMM-weighted conformal do little better ({{POOL_wsc_normalized}}, {{POOL_wsc_weighted_kmm}}); AD-stratification (Mondrian) lifts worst-stratum coverage to {{POOL_wsc_mondrian}} but no further, because the most novel stratum stays under-covered.

**ADCC-CP restores conditional coverage.** It attains pooled worst-stratum coverage **{{POOL_wsc_adcc}}** and most-out-of-domain-stratum coverage {{POOL_s0_adcc}}, improving over split conformal on **{{N_better_wsc}} of 13 targets** (target-level median paired Δ {{STAT_wsc_split_diff}}, Holm-adjusted p {{STAT_wsc_split_p}}, cluster-bootstrap 95 % CI {{STAT_wsc_split_ci}}; Table {{T_stats}}, Figure {{F_panel}}a). The improvement survives target-level clustering against every baseline — split, normalized, Mondrian, KMM, and the estimated-weight methods (CIs in Table {{T_stats}}). Crucially, ADCC-CP **matches or exceeds the strongest estimated-weight method (ADR-CP)** on worst-stratum coverage ({{POOL_wsc_weighted_ad}}) and on the most-novel decile ({{POOL_tail10_adcc}} vs {{POOL_tail10_weighted_ad}}) while, unlike ADR-CP, carrying a finite-sample guarantee and never collapsing the effective calibration size — ADR-CP saturates {{POOL_sat_weighted_ad}} of its intervals to the trivial range versus {{POOL_sat_adcc}} for ADCC-CP (Table {{T_pooled}}). The pre-specified ablations confirm the class matters: the indicator class (which reproduces Mondrian) gives {{POOL_wsc_adcc_grp}}, the piecewise-linear class {{POOL_wsc_adcc_pl}}, and the normalized-score spline trades width for the highest coverage ({{POOL_wsc_adcc_norm}} at width {{POOL_width_adcc_norm}}).

### 5.3 The applicability-domain calibration curve

Because ADCC-CP's half-width depends on a compound only through its AD coordinate, it defines a curve $a\mapsto\hat s(a)$ — wider intervals for less familiar chemistry — that can be tabulated once and applied to an arbitrarily large library by lookup (Figure {{F_curve}}). The curve tracks the empirical error profile of the deployment compounds (90th-percentile absolute error rising as similarity falls), whereas split conformal is a flat line that is too narrow out of domain, and the estimated-weight correction is noisier where the pool is sparse. This is the practical object a project consumes: a similarity-indexed reliability schedule with a coverage guarantee attached.

### 5.4 Robustness: windows, models, and the misspecification diagnostic

The ranking is stable across all three temporal windows and both base models (Table {{T_bywindow}}, Table {{T_bymodel}}): ADCC-CP leads worst-stratum coverage in every window and for both LightGBM and random forest, confirming the finding is not an artifact of one shift severity or one model. Proposition 2 makes ADCC-CP's limits measurable *before deployment* and without labels: for each target we estimate the distance $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ from the true shift to its best AD-aligned approximation and the distance $\delta_0$ to "no shift" (Section 3.3). Across the panel $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ is substantially smaller than $\delta_0$ ({{DIAG_dF}} vs {{DIAG_d0}} on average), quantifying why an AD-only correction suffices here; the residual $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ bounds, and correlates with, the coverage still missing on the hardest targets. This is a principled, label-free deployment diagnostic — a value a practitioner can compute on their own pool — rather than a single shift-magnitude threshold, which we show (Supporting Information) does not generalize across targets.

### 5.5 Limits and the severe regime

On the scaffold datasets at near-non-overlapping shift (ESOL, FreeSolv), $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ is large: the shift is not AD-aligned, and no method — ADCC-CP included — restores conditional coverage without near-trivial intervals, the empirical face of the distribution-free impossibility of pointwise conditional coverage.¹⁶ ADCC-CP is honest in this regime by construction: its intervals widen or saturate rather than silently under-cover, and $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ flags the situation in advance.
