## 4. Experimental Design

### 4.1 Data, splits, models

**Scaffold shift (development only).** Four MoleculeNet²² regression sets — ESOL,²³ FreeSolv,²⁴ Lipophilicity, and BACE²⁵ — split into 20 Bemis–Murcko scaffold-disjoint partitions (60/20/20). These were used *only* to select the ADCC-CP function class (Section 4.2) and never enter the confirmatory evaluation.

**Temporal shift (held-out confirmatory).** A panel of **thirteen time-stamped ChEMBL²⁶ targets across six biological families** — kinases (EGFR, VEGFR2/KDR, JAK2, SRC), a protease (BACE-1), an ion channel (hERG), a GPCR (μ-opioid receptor), nuclear receptors (androgen, estrogen-α, glucocorticoid) and enzymes (MAO-B, DPP-4, COX-2) — 2088–5163 molecules each, 1989–2025, each restricted to its dominant assay type (IC₅₀ or Kᵢ). Compounds are split chronologically by document year into three expanding windows (test = latest 40 %, 30 %, 20 %), giving mild-to-severe shift. Features are ECFP4 (2048 bits, RDKit³²). Base models are gradient boosting (LightGBM³¹) and random forest (scikit-learn³⁰); the conformal layer is a model-agnostic wrapper. Three calibration seeds per configuration. A leakage audit (chronological monotonicity, InChIKey de-duplication, training-only AD scores, label-free pool) passes for every target.

### 4.2 Pre-registration

The function class was chosen on the scaffold development data alone and frozen before any ChEMBL run (Supporting Information, Section S-adcc-pre). Among five candidate classes — indicator (Mondrian-equivalent), piecewise-linear, continuous spline, piecewise-linear with a local-difficulty term, and normalized-score — the **continuous linear spline** ($d=5$) was selected: it gave the best development-set worst-stratum coverage on two of four datasets with low saturation and the smallest dimension (hence the smallest $O(d/n)$ slack). The indicator, piecewise-linear and normalized-score classes are reported on the panel as pre-specified ablations; the local-difficulty variant ($d=9$) was not carried forward because the normalized-score class already represents local difficulty at lower dimension (all development results are given in the Supporting Information). The primary endpoint is worst-AD-stratum coverage at $\alpha=0.10$; secondary endpoints are coverage in the most out-of-domain stratum (S0), coverage on the most-novel decile, interval width, and saturated fraction. Statistics are computed at the **target level** ($n=13$): paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Holm-corrected) and cluster bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals over targets, never treating the {{NRUNS}} runs as independent, in line with recent recommendations for method comparison in molecular machine learning.²⁷

## 5. Results

### 5.1 The guarantee holds: validity controls

Before the shift experiments we verify ADCC-CP's finite-sample behaviour on controls where ground truth is known (Table {{T_validity}}). Under **exchangeable random splits** (no shift) every method is at nominal marginal coverage, and ADCC-CP is additionally at nominal coverage within every AD stratum (S0 coverage {{VAL_rand_s0_adcc}} at a 0.90 target), confirming it does not sacrifice validity for adaptivity. Under a **controlled shift with a known density ratio that is a piecewise-linear function of the AD coordinate** — inside ADCC-CP's class, so Theorem 1 applies exactly — ADCC-CP restores S0 coverage to {{VAL_plshift_s0_adcc}} whereas split conformal under-covers ({{VAL_plshift_s0_split}}). Under a known shift whose ratio is *exponential* in the AD coordinate — outside the class — ADCC-CP still corrects most of the shortfall, as Proposition 2 predicts. These controls demonstrate empirically what the theory asserts: coverage is maintained whenever the shift is AD-aligned, without any ratio being estimated.

### 5.2 Temporal shift: conditional coverage across thirteen targets

Under temporal shift, split conformal under-covers overall and severely for novel chemistry: pooled over the panel it reaches only {{POOL_marg_split}} marginal coverage, **{{POOL_s0_split}} in the most out-of-domain stratum** and {{POOL_tail10_split}} on the most-novel decile, at a 0.90 target (Table {{T_pooled}}). Normalized and KMM-weighted conformal do little better (S0 {{POOL_s0_normalized}}, {{POOL_s0_weighted_kmm}}); AD-stratification (Mondrian) lifts S0 coverage to {{POOL_s0_mondrian}} but no further, because the most novel stratum — where test compounds are more novel than any calibration compound — stays under-covered.

**ADCC-CP restores conditional coverage.** It attains pooled coverage in the most out-of-domain stratum (S0) of **{{POOL_s0_adcc}}** and on the most-novel decile of **{{POOL_tail10_adcc}}**, at a 0.90 target, versus {{POOL_s0_split}} and {{POOL_tail10_split}} for split conformal. On the *primary* endpoint, worst-stratum coverage, it reaches {{POOL_wsc_adcc}} (split {{POOL_wsc_split}}). Against the four stratified and kernel-weighted baselines the improvement is significant on every coverage and calibration metric and survives target-level clustering: versus split, normalized, Mondrian and KMM-weighted conformal, ADCC-CP raises S0 coverage by +0.095 to +0.194 (13/13 targets, Holm p = 0.0015 each) and worst-stratum coverage by +0.040 to +0.095 (11–13/13, Holm p ≤ 0.033), while *reducing* the mean absolute deviation from nominal across strata by 0.05–0.13 (0/13 worse; Table {{T_stats}}, Figure {{F_panel}}a). It also significantly beats the within-stratum guaranteed method MW-CP on worst-stratum coverage (+0.016, 13/13, p = 0.0015) and on the novel decile (+0.031, 11/13, p = 0.0024).

**Comparison with the strongest estimated-weight method.** The hardest comparison is ADR-CP, the one-dimensional estimated-weight method that led our preliminary benchmark. ADCC-CP covers the compounds that matter most **better** than ADR-CP: on the most-novel decile it improves by +0.030 (12/13 targets, Holm p = 0.0015) and on the S0 stratum by +0.020 (10/13, n.s.), with essentially identical interval width ({{POOL_width_adcc}} vs {{POOL_width_weighted_ad}}) and slightly better overall stratum calibration (gap −0.012, Holm p = 0.08, not significant). ADR-CP attains a higher *aggregate* worst-stratum coverage ({{POOL_wsc_weighted_ad}} vs {{POOL_wsc_adcc}}); inspection shows this is because ADR-CP lifts an interior stratum by *over-covering* (its coverage exceeds 0.90 in several strata), not because it covers the novel compounds better. The decisive difference is qualitative: ADCC-CP achieves this **with a finite-sample guarantee and no density-ratio estimation**, whereas ADR-CP plugs in an estimated ratio whose validity is not guaranteed and which is unstable where the pool is sparse. The pre-specified ablations confirm the function class drives the result: the indicator class (which reproduces Mondrian) gives S0 coverage {{POOL_s0_adcc_grp}}, the piecewise-linear class {{POOL_s0_adcc_pl}}, and the normalized-score spline {{POOL_s0_adcc_norm}} at higher saturation ({{POOL_sat_adcc_norm}}).

### 5.3 The applicability-domain calibration curve

Because ADCC-CP's half-width depends on a compound only through its AD coordinate, it defines a curve $a\mapsto\hat s(a)$ — wider intervals for less familiar chemistry — that can be tabulated once and applied to an arbitrarily large library by lookup (Figure {{F_curve}}). The curve tracks the empirical error profile of the deployment compounds (90th-percentile absolute error rising as similarity falls), whereas split conformal is a flat line that is too narrow out of domain, and the estimated-weight correction is noisier where the pool is sparse. This is the practical object a project consumes: a similarity-indexed reliability schedule with a coverage guarantee attached.

### 5.4 Robustness: windows, models, and the misspecification diagnostic

The ranking is stable across all three temporal windows and both base models (Table {{T_bywindow}}, Table {{T_bymodel}}): ADCC-CP leads coverage of the most out-of-domain compounds (S0 0.867–0.897) in every window and for both LightGBM and random forest, confirming the finding is not an artifact of one shift severity or one model. Proposition 2 can also be evaluated *before deployment* and without labels: for each target we estimate, from the calibration set and the unlabeled pool, the distance $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ from the (estimated) shift to its best AD-aligned approximation and the distance $\delta_0$ to "no shift" (Section 3.3). On every one of the thirteen targets $\delta_{\mathcal F}<\delta_0$ ({{DIAG_dF}} vs {{DIAG_d0}} on average; Table S-adcc-diag), i.e. a measurable part of the temporal shift lies along the AD axis — the part ADCC-CP corrects without estimation. We stress two limits of this diagnostic. First, the absolute values are large, so Proposition 2 is a conservative worst-case bound here rather than a sharp prediction of coverage; the observed coverage is far better than the bound. Second, across targets $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ does not rank the residual S0 shortfall of ADCC-CP (Spearman $\rho=0.05$, $p=0.86$, $n=13$). It is therefore best read as a label-free check that AD-aligned correction is relevant for a pool, not as a per-target coverage forecast — consistent with the preliminary finding that a single shift-magnitude threshold does not generalize across targets (Supporting Information, Section S-prior).

### 5.5 Multi-coordinate ADCC-CP

Extending the function class to two applicability-domain coordinates — structural and physicochemical (Section 3.6) — further improves coverage of the most out-of-domain compounds at a small width cost, confirming that chemical novelty is not fully captured by one axis. Pooled over the panel, ADCC-CP-2D raises S0 coverage to **{{POOL_s0_adcc_2d}}** (versus {{POOL_s0_adcc}} for the one-coordinate method) and the most-novel-decile coverage to {{POOL_tail10_adcc_2d}}, at mean width {{POOL_width_adcc_2d}} (versus {{POOL_width_adcc}}); the guarantee is unchanged because $\mathcal F_{\rm AD}\subset\mathcal F_{\rm AD}^{(2)}$. This is offered as a principled generalization — additional novelty axes (including learned embeddings) fold in identically — not a required default: the one-coordinate method already captures most of the benefit, consistent with the small physicochemical component of the temporal shift measured by $\delta_{\mathcal F}$.

### 5.6 Deployment: a per-compound reliability schedule for prospective screening

The practical payoff is a deployment object a project can use directly. In a prospective campaign a team scores an unlabeled library, ranks it by predicted potency, and synthesizes the top few percent — which are disproportionately novel, hence exactly where split conformal's intervals silently under-cover (Section 5.2) and where recent JCIM work finds uncertainty estimates least reliable.³⁸ ADCC-CP turns the calibration set plus the library into an **applicability-domain calibration curve** (Section 5.3): a lookup table mapping a compound's similarity-to-training to a guaranteed-coverage interval half-width, computed once and applied to a library of any size. A chemist reads off, per candidate, an honest interval whose coverage holds under the AD-aligned shift the library embodies — wide for genuinely novel chemotypes, tight for familiar ones — and the label-free $\delta_{\mathcal F}$ and $\delta_0$ statistics (Section 5.4) provide, before any synthesis, a check that the library's shift has an AD-aligned component for this correction to act on. This replaces a single global interval, or an unquantified applicability-domain flag, with a calibrated, inspectable, per-compound reliability schedule — the form in which uncertainty is actually consumed in a design–make–test cycle.

### 5.7 Limits and the severe regime

On the scaffold datasets at near-non-overlapping shift (ESOL, FreeSolv), test chemistry barely overlaps the calibration set, so the shift cannot be described as a tilt along the AD axis, and no method — ADCC-CP included — restores conditional coverage without near-trivial intervals, the empirical face of the distribution-free impossibility of pointwise conditional coverage.¹⁶ ADCC-CP is honest in this regime by construction: its intervals widen or saturate rather than silently under-cover, and the saturated fraction it reports makes the situation visible to the user.
