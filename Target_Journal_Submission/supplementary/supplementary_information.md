# Supporting Information

**Applicability-Domain–Aware Conformal Prediction for Molecular Property Regression under Covariate Shift**

All numbers are generated by the released code (`python run_all.py --mode full`) from the machine-readable files in `results/`. Section references are to the main text.

## S1. Per-dataset scaffold results and significance

Worst-stratum coverage (target 0.90; primary model LightGBM; mean over 20 scaffold partitions), ordered by shift magnitude (train-vs-pool discriminator AUC). Paired Wilcoxon vs split CP (20 partitions) in the last columns.

| Dataset | Shift AUC | Split | Mondrian | ADR-CP | ADE-CP | ADE-CP vs split (median Δ, p, rank-biserial) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lipophilicity | 0.65 (mild) | 0.822 | 0.872 | 0.825 | **0.875** | +0.052, p<0.0001, rb 0.99 |
| BACE | 0.76 (moderate) | 0.820 | 0.817 | 0.826 | 0.832 | +0.014, p=0.28, rb 0.31 (n.s.) |
| ESOL | 0.93 (severe) | 0.784 | 0.733 | 0.773 | 0.727 | — (no recovery) |
| FreeSolv | 0.98 (extreme) | 0.586 | 0.587 | 0.604 | 0.578 | — (no recovery) |

**Interpretation.** In the mild-shift regime ADE-CP significantly restores conditional coverage (and matches the strong Mondrian baseline); in the severe regime no deployable method recovers it. Pooled across all four scaffold datasets the proposed methods are *not* significantly different from split CP (median Δ = 0.000, Holm p = 1.0), precisely because the pooled average is dominated by the severe-shift datasets — consistent with the diagnostic law (Section 6.4). This is reported transparently; the scaffold datasets populate the hard end of the diagnostic curve, while the recoverable regime is demonstrated on temporal data (Section 6.3) and the controlled sweep (S4).

## S3. Model-agnosticism (scaffold, worst-stratum coverage)

Proposed methods are model-agnostic wrappers; results are stable across ridge regression, random forest, gradient boosting, and a fingerprint MLP (full table: `tables/table_by_model.csv`). Under temporal shift ADR-CP restores worst-stratum coverage to 0.878 (LightGBM) and 0.883 (random forest), vs. 0.683 / 0.719 for split CP.

## S4. Controlled shift sweep (diagnostic law, within dataset)

Shifting the test set along the first fingerprint principal component with a knob q∈[0,1] increases the measured shift magnitude monotonically and degrades achievable coverage, holding the dataset and model fixed:

| q | Lipophilicity AUC | ADE-CP worst-cov | BACE AUC | ADE-CP worst-cov |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0.57 | 0.88 | 0.61 | 0.83 |
| 0.25 | 0.65 | 0.86 | 0.69 | 0.85 |
| 0.50 | 0.76 | 0.87 | 0.78 | 0.83 |
| 0.75 | 0.88 | 0.82 | 0.89 | 0.71 |
| 0.90 | 0.95 | 0.79 | 0.96 | 0.70 |

This reproduces, with everything else held constant, the across-dataset diagnostic law (Spearman ρ = −0.56 for ADE-CP over all runs, p = 2.5×10⁻³⁴).

## S5. Weighted conformal is correct when the density ratio is known

The failure of high-dimensional fingerprint weighting (Section 6.2) is an *estimation* pathology, not a flaw in weighted conformal. In a controlled unit test (`tests/test_conformal.py::test_weighted_cp_restores_coverage_under_known_shift`), with train X ~ N(0,1), test X ~ N(1.5,1), heteroscedastic residuals, and the *exact* density ratio, split CP under-covers (coverage < 0.90) while weighted CP with the true ratio restores coverage to the target. This isolates the estimation problem and motivates the low-dimensional AD-axis estimators.

## S6. Data-leakage audit (summary)

`results/leakage_audit.json` (reproduced by `python experiments/leakage_audit.py`) confirms for every dataset: InChIKey de-duplication (unique = total), scaffold-disjointness (0 shared Bemis–Murcko scaffolds across train/calibration/test over three seeds), temporal monotonicity (max train/calibration year ≤ min test year), AD scores computed to the training set only, and covariate-shift weights estimated from fingerprints/AD scores without labels. All checks pass.

## S7. Hyperparameters and reproducibility

Features: ECFP4 (radius 2, 2048 bits). Models: LightGBM (400 trees, lr 0.05, 63 leaves), RandomForest (200 trees), Ridge (α=1), MLP (512–128, early stopping). Normalized-CP local scale: k=10 nearest training neighbours. Localized kernel bandwidth h=0.25. Discriminator: 32-component PCA + L2-regularized logistic (C=0.1) + isotonic calibration; probability clip [0.01, 0.99]; weight cap 50. AD strata: 4 quantile bins of calibration similarity-to-training. Seeds 0–19. Full configuration in `config/default.yaml`. The complete study (396 runs) completes in ~50 minutes on four CPU cores.

## S8. Scaffold paired-test table (transparency)

Pooled over the four scaffold datasets (`results/statistics.json::scaffold_primary_worstcov`), neither ADR-CP nor ADE-CP differs significantly from split CP on worst-stratum coverage (Holm p = 1.0), because the pooled average mixes recoverable (mild) and unrecoverable (severe) regimes. The recoverable-regime gains are established per-dataset (S1), on temporal data (Table 4), and in the controlled sweep (S4). We report the null pooled scaffold result rather than omit it.

# Supporting Information — Round 2 (expanded multi-target validation)

## S-panel. Per-target temporal panel
Thirteen ChEMBL targets across six families (Table 1), each split chronologically over three
windows (test = latest 40/30/20 %), three calibration seeds, LightGBM. Worst-stratum coverage
(target 0.90) per target and method is in `tables/table_panel_per_target.csv`; pooled values in
`tables/table_panel_pooled.csv` (Table 2). ADR-CP improves over split CP on **13/13 targets**
(median paired Δ +0.153, Holm p<0.001, rank-biserial 1.00).

## S-model. Model-agnosticism
`tables/tableS_model.csv`: the method ranking holds for LightGBM and random forest (ADR-CP pooled
worst-stratum 0.839 / 0.853 vs split 0.670 / 0.683).

## S-gnn. Graph neural network base model
`tables/tableS_gnn.csv`: with a D-MPNN-style message-passing GNN as the base model, ADR-CP again
leads (EGFR 0.819, μ-opioid 0.836 vs split 0.659 / 0.774), confirming the conformal layer and the
AD-axis advantage are model-agnostic.

## S-repr. Representation robustness
`tables/tableS_repr.csv`: the method ranking is preserved under an alternative fingerprint — ADR-CP leads under both ECFP4 (pooled worst-stratum 0.784) and FCFP4 (0.737), above split (0.704/0.691) and MW-CP (0.730/0.676).

## S-diag. Diagnostic validation (target-level)
`tables/tableS_diag.csv`: across targets, shift AUC does not predict achievable worst-stratum
coverage (Spearman ρ = −0.02, p = 0.96); leave-one-target-out false-safe rate 0.54. The universal
AUC-threshold rule is refuted; the within-dataset sweep relationship (strong) does not transfer
across targets.

## S-mwcp. MW-CP guarantee
Theorem 1 (group-conditional coverage) and proof are in Section 4; the assumption check
(within-stratum discriminator AUC) and the guarantee–efficiency trade-off vs ADR-CP are discussed
in Section 6.3. MW-CP is the certified option; ADR-CP is the empirically strongest deployable one.
